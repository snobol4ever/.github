# Seat conduct

The rules every seat is held to that are about the seat's own behaviour, not about a language or a layer. Each was paid for at least three times in the three-month history (DESIGN-PLAN skills-complete, the cio 2026-11-07, the history audit: fourteen recurring problem classes; eight of thirteen friction reports of 2026-11-04 named the kill and background rules). The law is the skills (RULES.md was triaged into them and retired, Lon 2026-11-11); this page is that law for a seat's own conduct with its sources. The bus, the rows and the sitting are skill `fleet-bus`; the box and its pools are skill `shared-box`.

## 1. Processes

- **Kill by pid, and only pids you spawned.** Never `pkill -f <pattern>`, never `pgrep` a pattern another seat shares: the pattern matched the caller's own shell three times (COO-341, COO-343, COO-432) and other seats' runs (Lon 2026-09-13, RULES.md FACT RULE). `harness/scripts/s4e_kill_mine.sh --handle <h> | --pid <pid>` kills what is yours with its children.
- **An orphan belongs to its seat.** A process reparented to the user manager with its parent gone is an orphan; the coo's fleet watch names it to its seat once an hour and never kills it (COO-462 ORPHAN_KILL=0, COO-463); the seat kills it by pid or says in one line that it is wanted. Orphans from killed parents filled a scope and stalled the box 2026-11-01 14:4x.
- **Background work only through a handle.** A job that outlives a tool call runs under `setsid` with its log and handle under `/tmp/s4e/<seat>/bg/`, reaped with the session; a bare `&` or a `sleep` left behind is the orphan of the rule above (the cio's own `sleep 3600`, 2026-11-07 09:12).
- **No seat but the coo runs a loop.** `/loop`, ScheduleWakeup and CronCreate are the coo's alone, and the coo's only when Lon starts it for an unattended fleet (Lon 2026-11-07 10:5x, verbatim: "It seems that seats believe it is okay to perform /loop command. That is forbidden and reserved only occasionally for COO when I run the fleet un-attended."; 2026-11-05 15:1x: "The COO is the engine with the /loop. The only seat with a loop."). Every other root's `.claude/settings.json` denies the two tools, except the cio's while Lon has the cio looping alone on the fleet redesign (Lon 2026-11-07 12:0x: "You better lift that ... now!!!"). A seat waiting on its own build or run parks its row `MEASURING:<seat>:<pid>` and stops; the bus or the coo wakes it (skill `fleet-bus` section 2). **A long poll is not a loop** (the cio's ruling, 2026-11-07 15:0x, on worker_05's ask; fleet-control step 12): when the Stop gate sends a live-set seat with nothing ready to `bash harness/scripts/s4e_msg.sh next --wait 240`, the seat runs it: that is the sitting's `next` holding the reserve open on the tube for 240 s, a wait on the queue (THE LADDER: a seat works what the queue serves it), not a scheduled wake, not `/loop`, not cron; it is the one way a seat stays on the queue without a message, and the gate sends it back as often as the tube is empty.
- **One process per identity.** The bus identity (`S4E_SEAT`) is held by one process; a bus verb holds the lock only for its write, never across a DONE-WHEN or a PREMISE run (CTO-502, fleet-queue step 19); a scheduled routine never runs in a seat root.
- **Heavy work goes through the doors.** A compile takes a cc slot through the Makefile (never a bare gcc), a run takes a run slot (`util_run_slot.sh`, the runners), a census or a sweep takes the batch class (`util_batch.sh`); nothing caps a seat but the one limit, `cores=N` of `CORES.conf` on `s4e.slice` (skill `shared-box` section 3, THE ONE LIMIT; the box-seatcap of 2026-11-07 is gone with every other quota). Speed is an instruction count; a wall clock under load measures nothing (skill `speed-measurement`).

## 2. Files and trees

- **Outputs under `/tmp/s4e/<seat>/`, never under the root**; the budget is 4 GB outside checkouts, idle ten minutes, swept (COO-474). **Worktrees live on /home** (officer_02 2026-11-13, row worktrees-live-on-home-and-hard-link-their-build; the coo MEASURED 14:3x CST /tmp, its own 63 GB filesystem, 87 to 90% full, 48 GB of it worktrees, and `new` refused box-wide): `s4e_worktree.sh new` makes `/home/resources/worktrees/<seat>/wt-<name>` (WORKTREES.conf `top=` names another, `--show-top` prints it), on a filesystem with about 144 GB free. A seat's worktree is a plain copy you can write. `--link` hard-links each clean file of its corpus, harness and .github to the read-only pristine (`util_relink_root.py`), and it is only for a tree that checks out and reads, the cycle's bases: MEASURED, a linked sparse worktree holds 379 MB of its own and shares 430 MB (24,908 files). In a linked tree the Edit tool and `sed -i` write a new inode (the link breaks, the pristine is untouched), but an in-place write (Python `open` with a or w, shell `>>`, `util_build_package_suite.py` and `util_suite_partition.py` writing ALL.csv and PARTITION.tsv) fails EACCES, so a seat never edits in one. `/tmp/s4e` stays the legacy top until it is empty: the cap counts it, the sweep sweeps it, `rm` finds a worktree there. A dirty root makes every reading unread (CEO-2060).
- **A root's tracked files may be shared, read-only links** (the cio 2026-11-12; skill `shared-box` section 4): corpus, harness and .github of a converted root are hard links to one pristine every seat reads. Never make one writable and never write one in place; a `Permission denied` on a tracked file in a root means the edit belongs in a worktree (`s4e_worktree.sh`).
- **An upstream checkout is never touched** (Lon 2026-11-12 19:0x CST, in the coo's window, verbatim: "Ensure you NEVER touch and upstream repo. EVER!" / "So we are clear, up-stream repo to me means a third-party repo we have cloned into /home resources." / "I suppose you could build one in place. I see no problem there."): an upstream repo is a third-party repository cloned into `/home/resources`. Its tracked files are never edited, and no git operation of any kind runs in it (no checkout, reset, stash, commit, clean or pull). Building in place is allowed: the build output a compiler or the oracle leaves beside the sources (XSB's `.xwam`, fpc's `.o`) is fine. A conversion, such as the semicolons a SCRIP Icon program needs, is a copy into corpus, never an edit of the original. Even a look writes: a plain `git status` refreshes `.git/index` (the coo's audit sweep of 2026-11-12 19:03 rewrote the index of five upstream Prolog clones, as officer_04 measured), so a read of an upstream clone is `git --no-optional-locks status`, or `find`, never a bare git verb.
- **A worktree is removed once its landing is on origin** (`s4e_worktree.sh rm <name>`, after checking that `git rev-list origin/main..HEAD` and `git status --porcelain` are empty in each repo). The box holds as many worktrees as `/home/resources/postoffice/WORKTREES.conf` says (`max`, 56 since 2026-11-13; 40 with no file), and `new` refuses, making nothing, when `/tmp` has under its floor free (`free_gb`, 8): the cio 2026-11-13, MEASURED 11:58 CST: the cap of 40, set for twenty juniors, was full, held by 25 of 37 seats, and 12 seats could make no worktree (`s4e_worktree.sh --show-conf`, `--probe-conf`). At 19:5x on 2026-11-12 the coo's 14 landed-but-kept worktrees filled the cap, and officer_02's proven cure could not land.
- **The sweep never removes a worktree a landing is using** (officer_04 2026-11-12 21:3x CST, MEASURED: the sweep that runs inside every `s4e_worktree.sh new` removed `/tmp/s4e/officer_04/wt-fusc` while `s4e_land.sh --home` ran its gate there, and the lander read a red gate with an empty `gate.log`): a worktree whose land lock (`/tmp/s4e/<seat>/land.<sha1 of the home path, 12>.lock`, held on fd 9 for the whole landing) is held is kept, and the sweep holds the owner's land lock while it removes one, so a landing starting in that moment refuses instead of losing its home (harness 40fc495b1; `s4e_worktree.sh --probe-sweep-land`). A seat's Claude shell keeps no cwd in a worktree between tool calls, so a worktree no baton names reads idle two hours after it was made, and one with nothing unlanded (no change, every commit on origin) is swept once idle 30 minutes, whoever owns it: make it with `--row <topic>` and it is never swept while that row is yours.
- **Generated tables are regenerated, never edited**; a rebase conflict in a registered generated output is resolved by regeneration (`s4e_land.sh` step 19, CTO-491); the skill gate refuses an edit of one outright.
- **The coo root's objects serve every other root** (`util_sync_root.sh`, alternates): no seat ever runs `git gc`, `git prune`, `git repack -d` or `git gc --prune` in a `/home/claude_coo/<repo>`; `gc.auto=0` stands there; a move, rename or re-clone of the coo root is a fleet event the cio rules on first (the cio 2026-11-07 09:1x).
- **Fold commits onto the base you started from, never onto a moved origin** (the grader 2026-11-12, MEASURED: harness 6f4e694ed was folded with `git reset --soft origin/main` after a fetch, so the commit carried the worktree's old copies of SLOTS.tsv and lib_slots.sh and reverted the coo's 8217eefc on origin until d68d7e4a7): squash with `git reset --soft "$(git merge-base HEAD origin/main)"`, read `git show --stat` before landing, and let `s4e_land.sh` rebase.
- **Cross-repo landings are one action through `s4e_land.sh`**: code repos first, `.github` last; never half a landing pulled or pushed; a hand push outside the lease is recorded by `s4e_push_rejected.sh` (skill `fleet-bus` section 7).

## 3. The record

- **The chat is not the record.** A ruling with a lasting idea lands in the skill it governs, on origin, the same sitting (the GOAL files are deleted, Lon 2026-11-11); what you did goes in the baton's LEDGER as you go; a condition told to a seat and never written was lost twice (RULES.md's two-modes note, CEO-935; hq_pascal's friction report 2026-11-04).
- **Every statement is MEASURED (the command beside it) or a HYPOTHESIS, marked**; a number with no command is a hypothesis whoever wrote it (the provenance law, skill `fleet-bus` section 4).
- **Time labels come from `date`, read before the label** (COO-194; the cio's two-hour drift of 2026-11-05); a label is `HH:Mx CST`.
- **A telegram is the record and wakes nobody; a SendMessage is a pointer** (CTO-469). `send` refuses a backtick or a command substitution in the body: write the command's name in words. An empty inbox is the acknowledgement; read, act or reply, clear.
- **No subagents in the repos** (Lon 2026-11-05); a reader spawned for a measurement runs read-only and its load is the seat's (section 1).
