## 1. What the literature says, in the order the eureka happened

- **Byrd 1980 (DEBUG.MEM § 1, pp. 2–9).** A predicate call is an *invocation box* — *"a different box for each different invocation"* — with four ports; *"for any invocation there is always only one call and fail, although there may be arbitrarily many redos and corresponding exits"*. The state a box carries between ports is the invocation's identity and depth, its current instantiation (*"when we redo a descendant we are in exactly the same place as when we left except for the direction of movement"*), the current clause (redo *"drops down onto the next clause"*), and the parent chain. Byrd's boxes NEST: the sub-goals of a clause are boxes drawn inside the second clause's box (p. 4); the whole run is *"a particular traversal of the and/or tree"*. He never boxes `,` `;` `->` (*"made invisible"*, p. 14) and has no `catch/3` (DEC-10 had none).
- **Proebsting 1997 (PLDI § 4, p. 2) — the eureka.** *"For each operator in a program's abstract syntax tree, translation produces four labeled chunks of code — one for each of Byrd's ports."* *"In the Byrd Box, ports are locations, whereas here they are pieces of code."* Start and resume are synthesized attributes, fail and succeed inherited; the machinery is *"nothing more powerful than conditional, direct, and indirect jumps"*. Per-activation state is the node's temporaries at fixed offsets, plus a runtime gate label where a connection is only known at run time (`ifstmt.gate`, `to.I`).
- **Proebsting 1999 (Jcon § 6.1, § 8.2.2).** A procedure that can suspend must keep *"enough state to enable subsequent resumption"* — Jcon copies temporaries, locals and the resume PC into a heap closure because Java has no code pointers; a non-generator stays a plain stack call. This is exactly the FRAME-PLACEMENT CRITERION's γ→β window: whatever must survive a suspend leaves the spine.
- **Danvy 2001.** The templates are *"intrinsic to the semantics … and can be provably derived"*: success continuations carry their own failure continuation (succeed carries resume), and defunctionalizing them yields *"stack-based run-time architectures"* — Proebsting's labels are the residual program of the semantics.
- **Kulaš 2003.** Every goal — `,` `;` `true` `fail` `=` included — is a box (Table 1); the 21 rules move an ancestor stack and a "bets" stack of bindings interleaved with `BY`/`OR` memos: choicepoints are NOT a separate structure, they are memos in the binding stack, one per open alternative.
- **Warren 1983 — the contrast.** The WAM keeps a code area, three stacks (local stack of environments + choice points, heap, trail), a PDL, and registers P CP E B A TR H HB S A1..An; choice points hold saved A registers, BCE, BCP, B′, BP, TR′, H′; environments hold CE CP Y1..Yn. The two-pointer local stack exists because a frame must survive its own return whenever a younger choice is alive — a plain LIFO call stack cannot express "exit but keep the frame". Byrd boxes solve the same problem the other way: the frame is retained at γ and released at ω.

**The synthesis the design was checked against:** a Prolog call is one retained-frame box (Byrd's invocation, Proebsting's four chunks); clause alternatives are the box's β; conjunction is γ→α forward and ω→β backward wiring; cut drops the resume ports to its left; `\+`, `->`, `once` are Proebsting's bounded `ifstmt`; catch is a box whose β inspects a ball; findall is `every` with "append and fail"; unification is a leaf whose β is its own undo (Kulaš unif:1–2); the WAM's B, TR, CP, BP and A registers become frame fields and wires.

## 3. Verdict

**The WAM is gone and the Byrd-box technique is the machine.** No solver loop, no instruction set, no choice-point stack, no environment stack, no PDL, no Prolog-named global; the choice point, the continuation, the cut barrier and the trail mark are frame fields of the call box; the ball rides a register; conjunction and disjunction are wiring; the dynamic database is code re-emitted on assert. Proebsting's eureka is realised literally — the ports are pieces of code — and the boxes are the same 134 templates Icon and SNOBOL4 use.

**Three residues violate "lists and stacks inside the box, no globals":**

1. **Argument staging through a global** — `g_call_args[64]` (`src/runtime/rt/rt.c:738`; written inline by `bb_call_proc_staged.cpp:94-110`, read by `rt.c:774-780`). This is the WAM's A-register file in all but name, shared by Icon, Raku and Prolog. Neither sovereign page sanctions it (§ A.1 lists `F.A[i]` in the frame and is silent on the path there). Hazard: any nested call between stage and install clobbers the block. **Cure shape:** stage into the callee's frame slots (the callee's `F.A[i]` is at a known offset once the frame is carved) or into the caller's spine, never a global — a shared-node change owed to all three frontends.
2. **The trail arena is outside every frame** — `src/runtime/rt/rt_pl_trail.c:7-14`, one 2^25-byte arena for the process, top in r12. `ARCH-PROLOG-THREE-ZETAS.md` § 2 rules *"no global trail area, no mark integer"*; `ARCH-PROLOG-BYRD-BOX-TRANSLATION.md` § A.1 / § B.11 records the r12 arena as Lon's *"second grant"* and the code follows it. The two sovereign pages contradict each other. The arena is not a global variable (no symbol; the only handle is r12) and the marks are frame fields, but the ENTRIES are the one Prolog list that lives outside the box that made them. **This is Lon's call**, not the ceo's: keep the grant (and reconcile THREE-ZETAS § 2 to it) or withdraw it (per-frame binding logs with heap spill, § B.11 (iii)).
3. **ISO errors that exit instead of throwing** — `pl_iso_uncaught` (`src/runtime/by_name_dispatch.c:1112-1118`) prints and `exit(1)`s for every builtin-raised ISO error (13 call sites, including an undefined predicate at `bb_call_proc_staged.cpp:236-237`): no ball, no r15, no unwind — a C escape hatch where § B.10 promises ω-with-ball. Already measured and half-cured by hq_R today (44 asm veneers for arithmetic, CEO-305 (1)); the census row `prolog-pl-iso-uncaught-call-sites-censused-with-witnesses-graded-through-catch3-never-off-stderr` (595, hq_T) grades the rest THROUGH catch/3.

**Two lesser residues:** `rtccb[32]` (`x86_asm.h:12`) parks the three wire registers in a GLOBAL around every runtime call — correct while no runtime service re-enters emitted code, and `rt_call_proc_descr` (`rt.c:906`, reached from `by_name_dispatch.c:641-697`) is exactly such a re-entry; and `rt_k_level` (`rt.c:402`), a global depth counter bumped by the shared spine helpers.
