## 4. x86_asm.h — 12f NOTED THE COUNT, NOT THE COMPOSITION; IT'S NOT UNIFORM AND SOME OF IT IS THE REAL RISK

12f named x86_asm.h's 25 occurrences as in-scope but didn't break them down. I did, since this file is different in kind from the template files above — it's the shared encoder, so anything genuinely live here runs **regardless of source language**, and is exactly the risk category the reachability argument in §1 cannot wave away. Three buckets:

- **Register-name string tables** (~9 occ): the `strcmp`-based name→number decoder and a disassembly name array. Inert text, not a use.
- **RTCC veneer writeback/reload block** (~14 occ, lines ~280-397): saves/restores all 9 caller-saved GPRs including r10/r11 into `g_rtcc_block` slots across a C-function crossing. This is not noise — it's the actual wire-*preservation* mechanism the WIRES charter's own W-6 rung is about ("RTCC's wire half... safe config = RTCC-ON and wire capture/restore, neither alone"). Worth treating as a preserver akin to `emit.cpp`'s existing whitelist entry, not a sweep target — but that's a W-6/whitelist decision, not one I made here.
- **Three genuine landmines, confirmed against 12f's independent sighting of the same mechanism from bb_call_fn.cpp's side (§3 of 12f):** `x86_store_cursor_mirror()` hardcodes `mov [r10],r14d`, and its parser counterpart at `x86_parse` maps the literal operand string `"[r10]"` to `XK_R10MIR` (store-only, no load arm) — this is the definition-side view of the same landmine 12f found from the *caller* side (bb_call_fn.cpp's own comments explaining why the author routed around it by choosing r11 over r10 in one spot). Two independent readings, same conclusion, worth trusting more for the agreement. Additionally: an indirect-call stub pattern appears twice (`movabs r10,ptr; call r10`, comment: "R10 already written-back so it is free at this point") that assumes r10 is dead immediately after RTCC writeback — a *third* hidden assumption that a future wire flip would falsify, since the wire's live value would already be sitting in the writeback slot, not garbage, at exactly the point this reuses the register. And one more at line ~1685-1687, an anchor-pointer load through r11 with a comment asserting "r11 is caller-saved and dead at every site" — worth checking against the wire convention specifically because the comment states its assumption so plainly.

None of these three are part of W-0's "sweep" in the classic sense (they're encoder internals, not template scratch) — but they're the concrete, itemized version of what 12f's own §6 named as underexplored scope, and they matter more than the 226-occurrence headline number because they can't be waved away by reachability.
