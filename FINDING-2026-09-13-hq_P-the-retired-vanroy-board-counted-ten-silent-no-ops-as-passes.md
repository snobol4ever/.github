# FINDING — the retired van Roy board counted TEN silent no-ops as passes

**Seat:** hq_P (HQ-PERFORMANCE, CONCERN 2 SPEED) · **Date:** 2026-09-13 · **Row:**
`bench-kernels-are-not-pristine-and-carry-no-refs-ceo-567-conversion` · **Authority:** CEO-567, CEO-582
(dark-is-worse-than-red) · **Tree:** SCRIP `ddfe8159e`, corpus `1b6d3724e`, `.github` `55293c37`, RT_OPT `-O0`

## The claim

`corpus/benchmarks/prolog/vanroy/` held 21 checked-in loop-wrapped kernels. **Ten of them exited `rc=0`
while printing ZERO lines of output** — they never reached the computation at all. Two gates graded that
directory by exit code, so all ten were counted as **passes**.

## The measurement

Every `vanroy/*.pl` run under `./scrip` (mode 3), exit code and output-line count captured without a pipe:

| bucket | n | kernels |
|---|---|---|
| `rc=0`, **zero output lines** | **10** | cal derive deriv divide10 fib log10 ops8 sendmore tak times10 |
| `rc=0`, output present | 4 | nrev(1438) qsort(1338) queens(10) zebra(11) |
| `rc=139` SIGSEGV | 7 | crypt ham meta_qsort mu nreverse queens_8 query |

The ten are exactly the ten kernels whose pre-conversion `bench/` sources carried the `wall_us` bracket.
The wrapped form was generated from those sources, so each one raised `existence_error(wall_us/1)` at its
first goal — **SCRIP registers `wall_us` at arity 0 only, unreachable from Prolog at any arity** — printed
nothing, and exited 0. `test_gate_prolog_vanroy_kernels_m3.sh` and `test_gate_vanroy_prolog_acceptance.sh`
both key on exit status, so a program that did nothing was indistinguishable from one that did the work.

## Why it survived

It is CEO-582 dark-is-worse-than-red, one layer below where that law has been applied so far. The boards
were not wrong about anything they printed; they were **complete, plausible and full**. The acceptance
gate even reported a healthy CLEAN=14 of 21. Nothing in either board's output could have revealed that ten
of those fourteen were no-ops, because the only evidence would have been an output volume neither gate
looked at. ⭐ **A pass criterion strictly weaker than the claim it is read as making will not fail — it
will report success, in detail, forever.**

It also compounded: `nrev` and `qsort` exit 0 having printed 1438 and 1338 lines where N=65536 and 16384
were asked for. They stop early and still exit 0, so even two of the four "real" passes were truncated
runs scored as clean.

## What changed

The directory is retired (its 21 names and 21 N values moved verbatim into
`corpus/benchmarks/prolog/fixed-iter-n.tsv`; the wrapped form is generated by
`scripts/bench_prolog_wrap.sh --mode=iter`). Both gates now generate the counted form from the **pristine**
`bench/` sources through `gen_counted_set()` in `lib_prolog_bench.sh`, so all 21 execute for real. On the
same tree and box, before → after:

| gate | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| `..._vanroy_kernels_m3.sh` | 21 kernels, 7 non-zero-exit, **RED** | 21 generated, 12–14 non-zero-exit, **RED** |
| `..._vanroy_prolog_acceptance.sh` | CLEAN=14 REFUSE=0 CRASH=7 other=0, **FAIL** | CLEAN=7 REFUSE=0 CRASH=13 other=1, **FAIL** |

⛔ **The crash count rising is the cure, not a regression.** Like-for-like, genuinely-clean kernels went
**4 → 7**: before, only 4 of 21 ran and printed at all. The verdict of both gates is unchanged (RED before,
RED after) and the acceptance gate's pinned `CLEAN_FLOOR=3` is still met at 7.

⛔ **The non-zero-exit count is not a stable number** — 12 on one run and 14 on the next, on an unchanged
binary. That matches this suite's own recorded behaviour (11 of 21 kernels flip between reps), which is why
the acceptance gate grades worst-of-3 and why no single reading of it may be quoted as *the* number. The
reproducible claim in this finding is the census table above, not either gate's count.

## What it does not say

The 13 crashes are **not** a benchmark defect. They are the Prolog runtime's iteration class already filed
on this row: ten kernels pass single-shot and die under iteration at a fixed per-kernel count that `-s`
does not move, as a raw SIGSEGV that bypasses the ERROR 246 guard in `src/runtime/rt/rt_stack_overflow.c`.
Owner is the Prolog runtime lane (hq_V / cto-hq_R), not this seat. `tak` is the one `other`: ERROR 246,
already routed as Prolog ISO ladder rung 9.

## Transferable

⭐ **Grade on the observation, never on the exit code, whenever the thing being graded is "did this run".**
An exit code answers *did the process end badly*, which is a strictly narrower question than *did the work
happen* — and, like `command -v` for an oracle and a `grep` for a leaf, it will not tell you it answered a
narrower one. `lib_prolog_bench.sh`'s `loop_check()` already applies the cure for the angle harnesses (N
copies of the `.expected`, byte-compared); these two gates do not yet, and that is a named remaining gap.
