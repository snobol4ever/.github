## THE LADDER RECIPE — writing and walking, optimized (hq_T owns the standard; the lanes walk it)

1. **The rung list is a census of the public reference, never an invention.** One rung per construct topic in the order the language's own book teaches it — SNOBOL4 the green book chapters, Icon the Icon book, Prolog ISO §7–9 as § E already lays it out, Raku the roast S-numbers, Pascal ISO 7185 §6, Snocone its paper, Rebus its manual. hq_T writes each census as `corpus/tests/<lang>/config/LADDER.tsv` (rung, slug, chapter, state) — declared, never derived (the `MODES.tsv` precedent); its last line IS the top rung, and the grid's L cell is 100% when `test_<lang>_ladder.sh --to <top>` is green in both modes.
2. **A rung is tiny and oracle-cut.** ≤ 20 lines, one construct, its ref cut from the oracle (never hand-typed), failing once before it lands (a corrupted ref must read FAIL). Rungs live in ONE `ladder.<ext>` + `ladder.ref` at the suite root, banner-delimited, paired by the builder in a scratch tree (hq_T 2026-09-03: a family is one source file, not a directory; a torn pair took every SNOBOL4 board on the box down for 40 minutes).
3. **Suite reds feed the ladder; walking the ladder cures the suites.** Every red a master or package suite names is ablated to a minimal witness and filed under the rung of the construct it exercises (`RUNG:<lang>/NN` on the row's LINKS line); a construct no rung covers becomes the NEXT rung. The ladder grows from the suites the public already knows, and a lane's order of work is its rung order — the `*-censused-by-class-and-cured` rows are where this happens.
4. **Rung N+1 opens when rung N is green in both modes; rungs on disjoint function families run in parallel** (the T ladder's seams table is the precedent). That is how sixteen seats walk seven ladders without a register or template collision: one seat per rung, one rung per seat, and a shared-node change goes to hq_C.
6. **Minting and walking alternate, rung by rung — never a whole ladder written first, never a cure waited on** (Lon ~18:30: *"I suppose you plan on minting and walking the feature rung ladders simultaneously or rather back and forth."* — yes, back and forth): the walker mints rung N from the census (witness + oracle ref, proven to fail once against a corrupted ref), runs it in both modes; GREEN → rung N+1; RED → the witness IS the class row for the HQ (Opus) and the walker keeps minting ahead on the next disjoint family while the HQ cures behind it. The ladder ahead of the current rung is the census; the ladder behind it is the regression floor (`--to N-1` stays green on every push). hq_T's rung-0–5 skeleton comes first only where no ladder exists yet (icon, snocone, rebus).
5. **One runner body** (`lib_ladder.sh`) serves seven stanzas: a runner fix lands for all seven at once; `test_<lang>_ladder.sh --to <top>` is the lane's landing gate on every push; SNOBOL4 FAIL=0 and Icon's watermark stay the control arms on every push regardless of lane. ## HOW THE FLEET WALKS (the rules; the ladders are below)

1. **A rung is a row.** Every unit of fleet work under this plan is a `QUEUE.tsv` row with a baton under `tasks/`, and every such row is exactly one rung of exactly one ladder. A row of ANY language on no ladder is an ORPHAN (2026-09-03; Prolog-only before) the owning HQ places or retires the same sitting (V6).
2. **Rank encodes the ladder.** Rank 0 = the CURRENT rung(s) of a ladder (marked ⭐ below); rank 1 = every other rung; rank ≥ 2 = off-ladder work. `next()` serves the topmost FREE row, so a seat that runs `next` steps onto a rung by construction. The ceo re-ranks each sitting from the walk (V4).
3. **A seat on an off-ladder row** lands it if its DONE-WHEN is within reach this sitting; otherwise pushes its progress, writes the LEDGER/NEXT, `unclaim`s (or `park`s on a real blocker) and runs `next`. Nobody rips a dirty tree (V2 is a notice, not a purge).

A rung marked `TO-MINT:<lane>` is a mint the lane OWES (V1).
5. **Rung exit = the row's DONE-WHEN.
