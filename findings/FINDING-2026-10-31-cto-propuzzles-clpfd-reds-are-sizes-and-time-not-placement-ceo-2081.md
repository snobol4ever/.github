# FINDING 2026-10-31 (the cto, CEO-2081): ProPuzzles' eighteen library(clpfd) reds are a SIZES-AND-TIME class, not a placement or reclaim class

The ceo's question (CEO-2081, 12:3x CDT): eighteen of ProPuzzles' twenty-two reds load library(clpfd) and fail on stack, hang or time, never on a wrong answer; euler10, euler23 and euler24 raise stack errors that a 256 MB stack does not change. Name the cause class, the witness to measure it by, and whose lane builds it.

## MEASURED (SCRIP c66534734 mode 3, the box at load 40-60, so every wall time is a bound and not a number; hakank_swi_euler10 from corpus/packages/prolog/puzzles, run from the package directory so hakank_utils and euler_utils load)

1. **At the graded sizes (ALL.csv: heap_kb 131072, stack_kb 4096) the error is the HEAP cap, not the machine stack.** The program prints `Warning: directive raised: error(resource_error(stack),_G0)` after about 37 s (the grader's reading, results.tsv 2026-10-30, FAIL in both modes, rc 0). That term is raised by `rt_pl_cap_error` (src/runtime/unification.c 3870), the GC poll's cap raise when a mark is still due after a collection (gc_heap.c 281): SWI's name for its global stack is our heap, and hakank's own source says what the program needs: `set_prolog_stack(global, limit(10_000_000_000))` before `primes(2_000_000, L)`. A 256 MB machine stack cannot change a heap cap, which is why hq_prolog's experiment read unchanged.
2. **With the heap at 2048 MB the 4 MB machine stack overflows:** `ERROR 246 -- stack overflow (unbounded or too-deep recursion exhausted the call stack)` at 227 s, RSS 929 MB. A non-tail recursion over a list of two million elements costs depth times frame bytes whatever the frame model does; swipl pays it on its 1 GB default local stack.
3. **With the heap at 2048 MB and the stack at 256 MB the run exceeds 600 s at 1.9 GB RSS** with no error and no output: a time class on a million-element workload (findall over about 4.8 million J2 candidates, sort, numlist to 2,000,000, delete_all over it, sum_list), the collector included.
4. **The frame model is NOT the class for the tail loops:** `numlist(2, 2000000, L), length(L, N)` runs at -s4m (177 MB RSS, 5.8 s under load) exactly as at -s64m; a tail-recursive prelude loop runs in constant machine stack today. Sampling euler10 under gdb at 5, 15, 30 and 90 s found the process still in the PLANNER (zd_pdef_fill, planner_mark_pass, zls_slot_matches on 15,218-node clpfd graphs), i.e. library(clpfd)'s compile time under load is itself a large share of every one of the eighteen's clock, and a hang graded under a time limit may be compile time before the program runs.

## THE CLASS, NAMED

Three facts in one class, none of them placement, the trail, or attribute-chain reclaim: (a) the declared sizes of the eighteen rows are the sizes of a hello program, where the programs declare their own need in their first lines (CEO-1353: the declared size is a HARD CAP, and the ruled cure for out-of-stack is a bigger declared size on the attribute row, never data moved); (b) under sizes that fit, deep non-tail recursion over million-element lists needs a machine stack in the hundreds of MB, as it does under swipl; (c) under both, the run is a SPEED reading, and speed is CLOSED for Prolog under CEO-1914. A hang at the bound (euler40, euler41, heterosquare, sicherman_dice, war_or_peace) is (c) until measured otherwise, with library(clpfd)'s compile time inside the clock.

## THE WITNESS AND THE LANE

The witness is euler10g's own pipeline cut into its stages, each a two-line program with its ref cut from swipl: (i) `numlist(2, 2000000, L)` (green today, the control); (ii) the findall of the J2 candidates and `sort/2` of them; (iii) `delete_all/3` of euler_utils over the two-million list; (iv) `sum_list/2` over the primes. Each stage is run at the sizes hakank declares (-d10240m, the stack raised until ERROR 246 stops, the number recorded) and measured by instruction count (`util_instr_count.sh`, CEO-2035), against swipl's count on the same stage. The stage whose count is more than an order over swipl's is the one to name; the stage that overflows the stack at any size names a non-tail road the prelude writes where swipl's lists.pl is tail-recursive, which IS a correctness row (the prelude's shape, hq_prolog's lane). The lane: hq_prolog cuts the four stage witnesses and measures them (one sitting); the coo re-declares the sizes of the eighteen rows to what the programs declare for themselves (set_prolog_stack in their source, or 10 GB global where they say so) and re-grades them once, so the denominator reads what remains after capacity; what remains is a speed row per program, PARKED under CEO-1914 with its counts attached, unless the stage measurement names a prelude shape, which hq_prolog cures as correctness. Nothing of the eighteen touches the planner or the collector's reclaim until a stage count says so.
