## 1. What the literature specifies (read this sitting from `/home/resources/byrd-box-literature/`)

**Byrd 1980, *Prolog Debugging Facilities* (DEBUG.MEM, RECORD):** every procedure call is a box with four ports — *Call* ("initial invocation of the procedure … control passes through the call port of the descendant box with the intention of matching a component"), *Exit* ("a successful return from the procedure … control now passes out of the exit port of the descendant box"), *Redo* ("a subsequent goal has failed and … the redo port of the descendant box. An attempt will now be made to re-satisfy the procedure … the clause that last succeeded; or, if that fails, to …"), *Fail* ("a failure of the initial goal … control now passes out of the fail port of the box"). The boxes are combined in sequence; the ports are *locations* in the control flow. Byrd's model says nothing about how a port is implemented, nothing about registers, nothing about indexing.

**Proebsting 1997, *Simple Translation of Goal-Directed Evaluation* (READ whole):** "the technique generalizes the Byrd Box". Per AST operator the translation produces **four labeled chunks of code, one for each of Byrd's ports** (start = CALL, succeed = EXIT, resume = REDO, fail = FAIL) plus a temporary for its value; **the ports are pieces of code, not locations**, and the chunks are stitched at compile time with direct gotos (`plus.start: goto E1.start`, `E1.succeed: goto E2.start`, `E2.fail: goto E1.resume`, `E1.fail: goto plus.fail`). **The operator's own logic lives inside its chunk** (`E2.succeed: plus.value ← E1.value + E2.value; goto plus.succeed`; `LessThan`: `if (E1.value ≥ E2.value) goto E2.resume`). A port whose target is decided at run time uses **an indirect goto through a banked label** (`ifstmt.resume: goto [ifstmt.gate]`). "The new scheme requires nothing more powerful than conditional, direct, and indirect jumps." Function calls that generate values need "a mechanism for suspending and resuming a function invocation" — in the 1999 Jcon paper (§ 6 Generators, § 8.1.1) that mechanism is the retained closure with a Resume entry; in SCRIP it is the retained activation frame with the banked β (`ARCH-PROLOG-BYRD-BOX-TRANSLATION.md` § A.1).

**Proebsting on Prolog itself (1997 p.2):** "Like Icon, Prolog evaluates programs in a goal-oriented fashion. Unlike Icon, Prolog uses unification and backtracking to produce a sequence of substitutions. Nonetheless, their goal-directed evaluation mechanisms are similar in that expressions ('calls' in Prolog) are started, succeed or fail, and may be resumed." And on Byrd: "It appears that Byrd used the boxes to model control flow between calls within a single clause, but not to model the flow of control between clauses within a procedure, nor to model the control-flow in and out of a procedure."

**The other Prolog papers in the directory** (Ducassé 1994/1997/1998 Opium; Jahier 1999 trace models by continuation semantics; Kulaš 2000; Brayshaw 1994; Bowen 1982; Pereira 1978; Carlsson 1983) use the box for **analysis and debugging** — trace models, port events, abstract views — and add ports (SICStus/SWI *exception*, SWI *unify*, ECLiPSe *next/else*) that SCRIP's § A already maps; none of them specifies the machine below the ports. The specification for the execution model is therefore Byrd's four ports plus Proebsting's rule: **each port is a compile-time-wired chunk, the operator's logic is inside the chunk, and only a run-time-decided port goes through a banked indirect jump.** The measure of "meets" below is exactly that rule.

## 4. Is unification "exactly" SNOBOL4 pattern matching? (Lon's question, answered in chat and recorded here)

Yes in structure — Proebsting says so — and not in four places, none of which needs C: (1) the subject is a tree, so the head walk is linearized at compile time with a temporary per nesting level (the WAM's get/unify sequence; still boxes); (2) unification is bidirectional — when the subject cell is an unbound variable the box must *build* the structure and bind the variable (write mode), a run-time-selected arm inside every structure node, Proebsting's gate shape; a pattern node never writes its subject; (3) variables chain, so every step dereferences with an inline loop; (4) choice points are per call with a frame retained across γ and a conditional trail push, Icon's generator frame, not a node's own state. Same machine, one extra arm, one inline loop, one frame kind.

## 5. The r12 question

r12 is the trail top today (`rtx_plunify.s` CTX_TR; § B.11) and r12 is the CAS top in SNOBOL4 (`bb_match_begin.cpp:31,80`, `rtx_match.s`). It is not used *like* the CAS: the CAS is pushed, tested and unwound by emitted code and asm; the trail's push and test are C (`plw_bind`, `pl_tr_needs_log`), its unwind a C call at 4,154 sites, and its entries live in a separate 32 MB arena (`rt_pl_tr_init`, `PL_TR_ARENA_LG2` 25) where the CAS lives on the spine — the arena grant Lon withdrew on 2026-09-06 (CEO-313) and nobody landed (row 521, rank 6). Lon's *"make it so"* is the rank-0 trail row.
