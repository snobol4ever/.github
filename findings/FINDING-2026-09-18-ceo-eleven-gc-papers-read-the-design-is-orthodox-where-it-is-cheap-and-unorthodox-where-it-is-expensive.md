## 1. ⭐ WHAT THE FIELD AGREES WITH US ABOUT, SO NOBODY RE-ARGUES IT

**Safe points at allocating call returns is the standard answer, not an improvisation.** Diwan/Moss/Hudson (PLDI '92) § 5.3: *"Selecting gc-points in a single threaded environment is easy: all calls can be considered gc-points since all allocation is done via a call and hence collection will never be triggered at a non-call point."* Wilson (1992) § 6.4.1 calls this the **safe-points** strategy against **gc-anytime**, and names its advantage in the words our § 3 needed: *"the compiler is free to use arbitrarily complex optimizations within the unsafe regions between safe points, and it is not obligated to record the information necessary to make it possible to locate and de-optimize pointer values."* ARCH-GC § 3 is that, arrived at independently.

⛔ **BUT DIWAN'S SENTENCE CARRIES A FOOTNOTE THAT IS A PRECONDITION, AND IT IS THE ONE THING IN THESE PAPERS THAT COULD HAVE INVALIDATED § 3's COMPLETENESS CLAUSE.** Footnote 8: *"This is true only if allocation is done via a call, in which case we must include inline allocations as gc-points."* An inline bump-pointer allocation is an allocation that is **not** a call return, and § 3's *"the set of safe points IS the set of allocating call returns"* would be false in its presence. **MEASURED HERE, SCRIP `4e1683fa9`: we satisfy the precondition.** No emitted template bump-allocates: `grep -rn 'hp_cur|g_hp_cur|heap_top|alloc_ptr' src/templates/ src/emitter/` returns **zero**, and the single `alloc` hit under `src/templates/` (`bb_call_bool.cpp:24`) is a frame-layout diagnostic, not a heap allocation. Every allocation in emitted code is a runtime call. **§ 3's completeness argument holds, and it holds for a reason that must be re-checked if an inline allocation fast path is ever emitted — at which point the inline site becomes a safe point or the argument breaks.**

**Two more agreements, stated so they stop being open questions.** (a) **Stop-the-world is the right arrangement for us and the modern literature has swung back to it** — Zhao/Blackburn/McKinley (PLDI '22, LXR): *"regular, brief stop-the-world collections deliver sufficient responsiveness at greater efficiency than concurrent evacuation"*, citing Pizlo's measurement that the best concurrent copying collector still costs ~20 % in throughput and latency. We are not behind the field by being stop-the-world; C4/Shenandoah/ZGC pay for pauses we do not need to buy. (b) **Sliding compaction preserves allocation order and that is a locality win, not just a fragmentation cure** — Wilson § 2.3: *"the garbage spaces are simply 'squeezed out,' without disturbing the original ordering of objects in memory… the allocation ordering is usually more similar to subsequent access orderings than an arbitrary ordering imposed by a copying garbage collector."*

## 2. ⛔ WHERE WE ARE UNORTHODOX, AND WHAT IT COSTS

**2a. EVERYTHING-IS-A-DESCR REPLACES THE LITERATURE'S PER-SAFE-POINT SLOT TABLE, AND IT IS THE REASON OUR MAPS ARE 0.12 % INSTEAD OF 16 %.** Diwan emits three tables per gc-point (stack pointers, register pointers, derivations) keyed by return address, and § 5.2 reports the cost honestly: **45 % of optimized code size naive, 16 % after δ-main compression plus byte packing**. Agesen measured JVM stack maps at **57 % of bytecode size** uncompressed. Our F1 moves that information out of the table and into the cells, and the map degenerates to 32 bytes **per graph** — `util_gc_descr_cell_census.py` read **0.12 % static frame growth** over 86 benchmark programs (ARCH-GC § 6.7(h)). ⭐ **That is a real and large win and it should be said out loud: we are an order of magnitude cheaper than the scheme our design is otherwise modelled on.** The price is exactly what F2 already priced — spine growth where an 8-byte raw word becomes a 16-byte cell, ceiling 25 %, packed nearer 10 %. **The trade is table space for cell space, and the papers let us state the exchange rate rather than guess at it.**

**2b. ⛔⛔ MANDATORY SLIDING HAS NO GRACEFUL DEGRADATION, AND EVERY OTHER MOVING COLLECTOR IN THESE PAPERS HAS ONE.** Immix (Blackburn/McKinley, PLDI '08) evacuates **opportunistically**: *"if immix runs out of space, it stops evacuating. If an object is pinned by the application or is on a target page, immix simply marks the object, leaving it in place."* Nofl (Wingo, 2025) keeps the same property and names why it matters: *"because Immix does not rely on evacuation of any individual object for collection to succeed, it is well-adapted to run-times that require the ability to pin some objects in place."* Bartlett's and Detlefs' mostly-copying collectors pin the referents of ambiguous roots (Wilson § 6.2). **Every one of them can fall back to not-moving a particular object and still be correct.** Our rung 2 is *every live aggregate SLIDES*, and F0 forbids the fallback in any form or under any name. **THE CONSEQUENCE IS NOT THAT THE ORDER IS WRONG — IT IS THAT THE ORDER REMOVES THE SLACK THE FIELD USES TO SHIP INCREMENTALLY, AND THAT IS EXACTLY WHY § 6.2b's BLOB FRAME IS A HARD BLOCKER RATHER THAN A SOFT ONE.** An Immix-shaped collector would pin the blob frame's referents and move on; we cannot, so the scan cannot be deleted for any program containing a pattern until rung 2 types that interior. **Read § 6.2b's exclusion and F6 step 3's deletion criterion with this in hand: the exclusion is not a tidy-up, it is the one place where the no-pinning order and the delete-the-scan milestone are in direct tension, and it will not resolve itself.**

**2c. THE CALLEE-SAVED REGISTER PROBLEM IS REAL, IS OLD, AND HAS A THIRD ANSWER WE DID NOT CONSIDER.** Diwan § 3: *"Using the register pointers table requires additional information about which registers were saved at each call point, so that the register contents can be reconstructed as of the time of the call."* Agesen § 2 confirms it bit them too: *"they also handle callee-save registers, which require traversing the stack to find callers' register information in order to type a register saved by a callee; we also encountered that issue in implementing the scheme laid out by Diwan et al."* Wilson § 6.4.2 names the design space as exactly two options: **partitioned register sets** (some registers only ever hold traced values — cost: register pressure) and **variable representation recording** (the compiler annotates which registers hold pointers where — cost: annotations, and *"the space requirements for this additional information may not be negligible"*). ⭐ **§ 6.5c's spill record is a third point in that space and it is the cheap one: a dynamic partitioned convention enforced at the poll boundary only, rather than statically over the whole register file or annotated at every site.** § 6.5a's measurement — that the register facts are a property of the SITE and not of the GRAPH — is precisely why a per-graph mask (the Diwan register table shape) could not work here, and it is the measurement that justifies the divergence. ⛔ **AND THE FOURTH ANSWER, WHICH DISSOLVES THE CLASS ENTIRELY, IS OCAML'S: there are no callee-saved registers in the OCaml calling convention at all** (Sivaramakrishnan et al., PLDI '21, § 2.1), which is what makes their external calls and exception handling cheap. **Not a proposal — SCRIP's templates are built on r12–r15 as named planes — but it is the reason their stack maps carry no register half, and anyone reading their design should know that before copying its simplicity.**

## 3. ⭐ WHAT THE PAPERS EXPLAIN ABOUT MEASUREMENTS WE ALREADY TOOK

**3a. THE cfo's 400 MB → 290 MB RETENTION FINDING (CFO-872) IS BOEHM/WEISER'S CENTRAL SENTENCE.** *"In contrast to a copying collector, such an error cannot result in incorrect program execution. It can however result in unnecessary memory consumption."* (SP&E 18(9), 1988.) A conservative sniff's misidentification is **invisible to every correctness witness and visible only in retention** — which is exactly the cfo's own self-correction (*"every witness I ran asked DOES ANYTHING BREAK, and none asked DOES ANYTHING CHANGE"*). ⛔ **AND THE MIRROR HALF IS WHY LON'S ORDER IS RIGHT:** the sentence only holds *because Boehm never moves*. A collector that moves on a conservatively-identified word does not leak, it corrupts. **The 1988 paper is the argument for the 2026-09-17 reset, written 38 years early.** ⭐ Boehm also measured his own conservatism: *"we typically saw 4 to 5 [near misses] per garbage collection of a 2 megabyte address space"* — in C and Russell, where most words are pointers or small integers. **Our setting is far worse and the papers predict it: a SNOBOL4 descriptor's `slen` and payload halves are arbitrary integers by construction, so our near-miss rate has no reason to resemble his, and the sniff retaining half a heap is the expected reading, not an anomaly.**

**3b. HEAP SIZING HAS A KNOWN CURVE AND WE SHOULD STOP GUESSING AT PACING.** Appel (SP&E 19(2), 1989) defines γ as the ratio of memory to live data and measures SML/NJ at **γ=3 → ~11 % GC overhead, γ=7 → ~6 %**; below γ=2 the collector cannot run. Nofl restates the same shape from the other end: *"giving a process 10 % more heap can cut its CPU overhead in half, at the tight end of the curve."* Wilson § 8's overall estimate for a well-implemented non-incremental generational system is *"roughly ten percent of running time … with a space cost of roughly a factor of two in data memory size."* **Our arena is a 512 MB default with an 8× virtual-reserve cap (F4) and no γ anywhere in the design. Before anyone tunes `test_gate_gc_pacing_bounds_a_churning_program`'s band again, the number to think in is γ, and the curve is steep and known.**

**3c. GENERATIONAL IS PARKED AND THESE PAPERS PRICE THE PARK.** Hosking/Moss/Stefanović (OOPSLA '92) is the write-barrier comparison: **card marking at 256–512 bytes is optimal on their hardware, remembered sets are best overall, page trapping is too coarse, and generation filtering is ineffective for cards**. Total GC overhead 1.5 %–6.9 % on real benchmarks, 16–37 % on a deliberately mutation-hostile one. Wilson § 4.4 puts the write barrier at *"the largest cost of generational garbage collection"* — twenty instructions per pointer store would cost ~20 % in a Lisp-shaped workload; a good card barrier is 3 instructions and ~4–5 %. LXR's modern field-logging barrier is **1.6 %**. **So the park costs us the generational win and saves us a barrier on every pointer store; when Lon unparks it, the barrier is the decision and card marking at 256–512 B is the literature's default, not a thing to re-derive.**

## 4. ⛔ THE TWO PAPERS WE DO NOT HAVE AND SHOULD

The README names Goldberg (*Tag-free garbage collection for strongly typed programming languages*, PLDI '91, pp. 165–176) and Stichnoth/Lueh/Cierniak (*Support for garbage collection at every instruction in a Java compiler*, PLDI '99, pp. 118–127) as the two worth another attempt, *"both bear directly on distinguishing a pointer from an integer"*. **Both are indexed in the Jones/Hosking/Moss bibliography (lines 2955 and 7824), so the citations are confirmed real and exact.** Stichnoth is the one that matters most for us: it is the gc-anytime end of Wilson § 6.4.1's axis, and it is the paper that would tell us what we are buying by having 97 of 210 allocating call sites still unpolled — that is, what the cost is of *narrowing* the safe-point set rather than widening it.

## 5. THE ONE THING NO PAPER HERE COVERS

**Coexpressions.** The closest analogue in the set is Sivaramakrishnan et al.'s fibers: heap-allocated stacks, 16 words initially, grown by copying to double. Their enabling invariant is one sentence and it is a hard constraint on any SCRIP coexpression design that ever wants to move a stack: ⛔ ***"Since OCaml does not generate pointers into the stack, the two `fiber_info` fields are the only ones that need to be updated when fibers are moved."*** A stack with interior pointers into it cannot be relocated cheaply. ⭐ Two mechanisms of theirs are directly reusable regardless: a **stack-overflow check in the function prologue with a fixed-size red zone so leaf functions elide the check** (the same shape as our poll, and the same argument for why it is cheap — *"these stack overflows are rare and so the overflow checks will be correctly predicted by the CPU branch predictor"*), and **running external C calls on the system stack** because C frames carry no overflow check. Their whole-system cost on code that does not use the feature: **1 % mean**.

⭐ **THIS IS THE THING THE PAPERS SPEND THEIR HARDEST MACHINERY TRYING TO RECONSTRUCT.** Diwan § 3–4 — half the paper — builds a derivations table keyed by PC, with base values, invertible operations and *path variables* for ambiguous derivations, because a C value has nowhere to carry where it came from. Agesen resolves the jsr problem by **rewriting bytecode to split a variable** whose type is path-dependent, because a JVM slot has nowhere to carry it. The two papers the README most wants and we do not have — Goldberg *Tag-free garbage collection* (PLDI '91, bibliography line 2955) and Stichnoth *Support for garbage collection at every instruction* (PLDI '99, line 7824) — **are both attempts to recover provenance from code that never carried it.** We carry it and discard it.

⛔ **THE IMMEDIATE CONSEQUENCE, RULED IN ARCH-GC § 8.2 AS CEO-898: `DT_DATA`'s overload (CFO-873) is cured by stamping `mint_op`, not by minting a tag code.** The cfo measured both forms at identical `v` and identical `slen = 0` and concluded *"indistinguishable from the descriptor alone"* — **they are not; `mint_op` is in the descriptor and is zero at both.** Three writers stamp, `gc_visit_one` branches, four readers tighten. It beats a new code because the stride-8 tag ladder has no free slot below `DT_ORDER` (0x08→0xA0 fully occupied; the only gap is in the value-never-a-pointer region § 6.1 reserves), because it is 3 sites against the block-side alternative's 49, and above all because **a missed writer reads `UNSTAMPED` and the visitor can REFUSE, where a missed writer under a new tag code emits the old tag and the typed walk dereferences it wrongly — the cfo's crash, again, silently.** ⛔ **Precondition the cfo must measure first, named in our own tree:** `bb_keyword_icon.cpp:34` records that word 0 is `{v:1,mint_op:1,src_node:2,slen:4}` packed and that *"a bare `mov ZRES(0),DT_S` zeroes slen along with it"* — **there is emitted code that writes the tag with a sub-word store and collaterally clobbers the stamp.** A stamp a template silently zeroes is worse than no stamp; that census is the fail-once.

⭐ **AND THE FOUR PORTS ARE A COMPILE-TIME CFG (CEO-899).** Measured: `x86_alpha/beta/gamma/omega` (`x86_asm.h`:510–516) are labels and jumps, used by **130 / 50 / 124 / 93** of the 136 boxes; string port operands were retired by Lon at s5. **The wiring IS the control flow and it exists in the emitter before anything runs — the object Diwan and Agesen must recover by dataflow over optimized code.** Two things follow. (a) ARCH-GC § 3's *"a loop that allocates passes a safe point every iteration by construction"* is an **assertion**, and both papers treat that property as the thing that must be proved — Diwan § 5.3 inserts a gc-point in every loop lacking one, Agesen § 3 makes backward branches gc points. **We can decide it instead: walk the α/β wiring for cycles and red any cycle with an allocating call and no poll.** That also tests whether `unpolled=97` means what it says — a count of call sites cannot tell you whether an unpolled site is reachable without passing a polled one, and the port graph can. (b) **ω, and a graph's single γ, are safe points whose live set is structurally empty** — at a concede the box's ζ is dead, so a poll there needs no § 6.5c spill record at all. The papers place polls where memory is *consumed*; the ports let us also place them where liveness is provably *minimal*, known by construction rather than by a backward liveness pass. ⚠ Not a substitute: the allocation already happened, so a port poll is a poll with a **bounded delay of one box's allocation** — which is exactly the latitude Rule 3 bought by making the allocator grow the window instead of collecting. **A sizing for the cto against the step-4 worklist, not an admission.**
