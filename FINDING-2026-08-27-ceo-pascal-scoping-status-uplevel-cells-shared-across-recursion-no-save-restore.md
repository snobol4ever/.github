## Design analysis under the BB FRAME-PLACEMENT CRITERION (RULES.md)

- **The spine is NOT unused — Pascal is the best-behaved spine client we have**: no γ-SUSPEND anywhere, strictly nested expressions, bounded compile-time-known growth between every RESULT's γ and its consumer — every temporary PASSES the fixed-offset test. Forth-style spine fits Pascal expressions perfectly.
- **VAR-declared variables are where the criterion bites**: a variable referenced by a nested procedure is loaded by an operator whose distance to the defining frame is unknowable at compile time (arbitrary recursion between definer and referencer) — FAILS the fixed-offset test → ζ-ACTIVATION-FRAME, reached via a re-established base. Recursive procs' own locals likewise need per-invocation cells (already true via varslots).
- **The zetas-native uplevel design**: a DISPLAY in ζ-STANDING — `display[level]` cells at fixed offsets from the standing base; proc entry at static level L saves `display[L]`, sets it to its fresh RBP; exit restores; uplevel ref = two loads (`display[L]` then `[base+off]`), both at fixed offsets from re-established bases — satisfies the criterion exactly, recursion-correct, zero new registers, zero claims. Pascal never needs the workspace-island frames (no escape; strictly LIFO) — unlike Raku, whose ESCAPING closures are Icon-generator-class (island frames, after icon-n2).
- **R13/R14/R15 register display**: the SPEED variant of the same design — but those registers are the pinned scan set (Σ/δ/Δ) in the shared codegen, and the dead r12 SL slot is the cautionary precedent for claiming a register before the mechanism is proven. Standing-display first (correct, simple, measurable); register display as a later measured perf rung if the two loads show in profiles, with the scan-set collision settled by save/restore convention at cross-language call boundaries.
