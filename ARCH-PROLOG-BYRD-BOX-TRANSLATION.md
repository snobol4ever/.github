## A. The port correspondence — Byrd's four ports, SCRIP's four ports, and what holds at each

Byrd (1980, RECORD; port names quoted from Kulas 2003 READ p.1 and SICStus 3.7.1 FETCHED) draws each predicate call as a box with four ports: *call* "entering the procedure in order to solve a goal", *exit* "leaving the procedure after a success", *fail* "leaving the procedure after the failure", *redo* "re-entering the procedure, i.e. another solution is sought for". Proebsting (1997, READ p.2) renames them start/succeed/fail/resume, footnotes *"Byrd called these call, redo, fail, and exit"*, and makes the decisive move: *"In the Byrd Box, ports are locations, whereas here they are pieces of code."* SCRIP's α/β/γ/ω are Proebsting's four chunks with Lon's names.

| Byrd | Proebsting chunk | SCRIP port | direction | trace line it produces (Byrd/DEC-10 form, SICStus 3.7.1 FETCHED) | invariant that holds when control is AT this port |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CALL** | `θ.start` | **α** proceed | into the box | `(N) D Call: p(A,B)` | frame F carved, `F.B0` = the youngest choice older than F, params `F.A[i]` staged, `F.LOG` empty, `F.CUR` = first candidate clause, no binding by F yet |
| **EXIT** | `θ.succeed` | **γ** succeed | out, forward | `(N) D Exit: p(a,b)` — ECLiPSe writes `*EXIT` when the box keeps alternatives (FETCHED) | the current clause's head+body succeeded; bindings made by F's activation are live and logged in `[F.B].LOG` (§ B.11); if F still has alternatives the frame is RETAINED (Proebsting: not `bounded`) and `F.RES` names the youngest sub-goal's β; if not, the frame is released and `F.LOG` was empty or promoted (§ B.11) |
| **REDO** | `θ.resume` | **β** recede | into the box, backward | `(N) D Redo: p(a,b)` | re-entry at `F.RES` (redo the youngest sub-goal) or, when that sub-goal is exhausted, a clause step: undo `F.LOG`, re-seed locals `F.G[j]` unbound, `F.CUR` += 1, unify the next head; the caller's frame is exactly as at the matching γ (the caller's own work since then has been undone by ITS log) |
| **FAIL** | `θ.fail` | **ω** concede | out, backward | `(N) D Fail: p(A,B)` | `F.LOG` fully undone, `F.CUR` exhausted, frame released, every choice younger than F dead; the wire carries `r10 = F.B0` so the caller's `F.B` reverts (§ B.3) |
| EXCEPTION (SICStus/SWI/gprolog, FETCHED — not in Byrd) | — | **ω with ball** (`r11 ≠ 0`) | out, backward | `(N) D Exception: p(A,B)` — SWI: *"activated on each parent frame ... until the exception is caught"* (FETCHED) | same as FAIL plus `r11` = heap copy of the ball; every box's ω passes `r10:r11` through untouched; only a catch box's β-handler inspects `r11` (§ B.10) |
| UNIFY (SWI only, FETCHED: *"the neck instruction, signalling the end of the head-matching process"*) | — | γ of the head-unification sub-box | inside the box | `(N) D Unify: p(a,B)` | head of clause `F.CUR` unified; body not yet started |
| NEXT / ELSE (ECLiPSe only, FETCHED) | — | β of the clause box / disjunction box that steps `F.CUR` | inside | `NEXT`, `ELSE` | as REDO's clause-step arm |
| cut_call / cut_exit (SWI `prolog_trace_interception/4`, FETCHED) | — | α / γ of the cut box | inside | — | `F.B := F.B0`, younger choices dropped, their logs promoted (§ B.6) |

**Where the sources disagree, and what this page picks.** (1) *What REDO means after a cut.* SICStus 3.7.1 (FETCHED): an invocation *"cut out of the search space by cuts"* is simply never re-entered; *"the system fails to the latest surviving Call port before the correct one"*. SWI adds `cut_call(PC)`/`cut_exit(PC)` events so the trace SHOWS the cut; ECLiPSe shows nothing. **Pick: SWI's** — the cut is a box (`IR_CUT`, `lower_prolog.c:688`), so it has an α and a γ and the hook fires on both for free; a REDO into a cut-away box is by construction unreachable (its token is dropped, § B.6), which is SICStus's semantics with SWI's visibility. (2) *The exception port.* Byrd has four ports; SICStus, gprolog and SWI draw a fifth exit. **Pick: no fifth port.** An exception is ω with `r11 ≠ 0`: same wire, same undo discipline, one extra register test at the catch box only — the trace prints `Exception` instead of `Fail` when the hook sees `r11 ≠ 0`. (3) *Box granularity.* Proebsting (1997 READ p.4): *"It appears that Byrd used the boxes to model control flow between calls within a single clause, but not to model the flow of control between clauses within a procedure, nor to model the control-flow in and out of a procedure."* Kulas (READ p.2, Table 1) extends ports to EVERY goal, including `A,B`, `A;B`, `true`, `fail`, `T1=T2`. **Pick: Kulas's** — every IR node is a box; the predicate-call box is the one that owns a FRAME; conjunction/disjunction/unify boxes are frameless (their ζ is the enclosing frame's, as the Icon lowerer already does for `&`, `|`, `not`). (4) *EXIT determinism.* ECLiPSe's `*EXIT` and SWI's *"It is possible for the goal to have alternative"* are the same bit Proebsting calls `bounded` and SCRIP calls `deterministic` (`IR.h:218`, stamped at `lower_prolog.c:1228`). **Pick:** the γ hook reports it from `F.CUR` (exhausted or not) — one compare, no new state.

### A.1 The frame — the one record every Prolog call box owns (the variable alignment, stated once)

The activation frame of a Prolog call box is Icon's frame with the WAM's environment AND choice point folded into it (Lon: *"the stacks and lists moved INTO the BB box"*). It is RBP-pinned after the carve, as hq_C landed it (`xa_flat.cpp:199-204`: `sub rsp,kt` · `[kt-24]=γ` · `[kt-16]=ω` · `[kt-8]=caller rbp` · `rbp=rsp`; caller-base restore through the pin `xa_flat.cpp:183`). The header sits at the BASE because `kt` differs per graph and a binder that only knows a frame's address must find `B`, `LOG` and `HI` at fixed offsets — the same reason today's `[fb+0]/[fb+8]` "already suspended" pair is base-relative (`bb_suspend.cpp:20-33`, `rt.c:1632-1643`).

**The B wire IS `r13` (Lon's grant above; read every "`r10`" in this paragraph as `r13`).** Every port transfer between frames carries the youngest-choice pointer in `r10`: α — caller hands `r10 = caller.F.B`, callee stores `F.B0 := r10`, `F.B := (more than one candidate clause) ? rbp : r10`; γ — callee hands `r10 = callee.F.B` (itself or something younger it retained; unchanged if bounded), caller landing stores `caller.F.B := r10`; ω — callee hands `r10 = callee.F.B0`, caller landing stores `caller.F.B := r10`; β — no wire needed, the retained callee's own `F.B` is still right (every choice younger than it died before the caller backtracked into it). This is the WAM's B register carried by wires and frames instead of a global — what Lon asked for. `rax:rdx` stay the DESCR return per THE PORT-EXIT VALUE CONTRACT; `r11` is the ball (§ B.10).

**What Icon already has and Prolog copies rather than invents:** the retained frame that survives γ (`emit.cpp:2899` α, `:3330` suspend keeps the frame, `:3255` landing repoints rbp and does NOT release); the resume label banked in the frame and jumped through by β (`bb_call_proc_staged.cpp:832-838`: `mov rax,[act+8]; mov rsp,[rax+24]; sub rsp,40; jmp [rax+32]`); the per-site callee token `callgen.act`; the `bounded` elision of β chunks (`irgen.icn:113-140` Limitation, `:167-200` Alt: `/bounded & suspend ir_chunk(p.ir.resume, …)`); the indirect-goto gate for constructs whose resume target is only known at run time (`irgen.icn:167-200` `ir_MoveLabel`/`ir_IndirectGoto`; Proebsting 1997 READ p.4 `ifstmt.gate`). What Icon does NOT have and Prolog adds: the binding log (Icon has no bindings to undo), the B/B0 pair (Icon has no cut), the clause cursor as a frame field (Icon's alternation cursor is a spine temporary because `|` never suspends across a call boundary the way a clause does).

## B. Per construct — port wiring, Proebsting shape, variable alignment

Notation: `X.α X.β X.γ X.ω` are the four chunks of box X. "Wire" = a compile-time `jmp` (`ARCH-ENGINE.md` § Flat-BB ABI). "Frameless" = the box has no frame of its own; its ζ lives in the enclosing call box's frame. Every subsection ends with the source whose wiring was followed.

### B.1 Fact `p(a, b).`
(i) One call box P. `P.α` → head-unify sub-box `U.α`; `U.γ` → `P.γ`; `U.ω` → next clause or `P.ω`. No β chunk when P has one clause (bounded). (ii) Proebsting's `literal` (1997 READ p.3): `start` produces the value and goes to `succeed`; `resume` goes to `fail`. Today: `lower_pl_clause_into` (`lower_prolog.c:718-738`) emits one `$unify` per argument (`:731`), a list-pattern fast path `$unify_lst` (`:727`, sunk inline at `bb_call_fn.cpp:232`), and `pl_fact_choice_lookup` (`:37-48`) recognises all-constant fact tables. (iii) `F.A[i]` params; scratch for the constant on the spine; `F.LOG` receives the binding of a caller cell to the constant (§ B.11) — and because a single-clause fact is bounded, `F.B := F.B0`, so the entry lands in the caller's youngest choice, exactly the WAM. **Source: Proebsting 1997 § 4.1 (READ); Kulas S:atom:1-3 (READ p.5).**

### B.2 Rule `p(X) :- q(X), r(X).` (head + body conjunction)
(i) `P.α` → `U.α` (head) → `Q.α` → `R.α` → `P.γ`; backward: `R.ω` → `Q.β`; `Q.ω` → (next clause | `P.ω`); `P.β` → `R.β` (the youngest sub-goal). (ii) Proebsting's `plus` (1997 READ p.3): `plus.resume: goto E2.resume`; `E2.fail: goto E1.resume`; `E1.fail: goto plus.fail` — conjunction is the binary-operator threading with the value replaced by the substitution. Today `thread_goals` (`lower_prolog.c:186-243`) wires exactly this, and records the RIGHTMOST resumable goal as the clause's β (`redo_out`, `:722`). (iii) `F.A`, `F.G` (locals X), `F.ACT[q]`, `F.ACT[r]` tokens, `F.RES` = R's β label banked at `P.γ` (Icon's `[H+32]`). **Source: Proebsting 1997 § 4.3 (READ); Kulas S:conj:1-6 (READ p.5): `fail B' → redo A`, `redo A,B → redo B`.**

### B.3 Predicate with several clauses — THE CHOICE
(i) `P.α`: `F.CUR := first candidate` (after § B.13 indexing), `F.B := rbp` if more than one candidate else `F.B0`; try clause `F.CUR`. `P.β`: if `F.RES ≠ 0` jump it (redo the youngest sub-goal of the live clause); else CLAUSE STEP: undo `F.LOG`, re-seed `F.G[*]` unbound (the `LCL-SEED` `rep stosb` precedent, `emit.cpp` LCL-SEED), `F.CUR += 1`, if exhausted → `P.ω` else → that clause's `U.α`. `P.γ`: bank `F.RES`, hand `r10 = F.B`, keep the frame. `P.ω`: `r10 = F.B0`, release. (ii) Proebsting's `Alt` in Jcon (`irgen.icn:167-200`): each alternative's `success` does `ir_MoveLabel(t, alt_i.resume); goto Alt.success`, and `Alt.resume: ir_IndirectGoto(t)` — the resume target is a TEMPORARY holding a label, and `alt_i.failure → alt_{i+1}.start`. Prolog's clause box is Jcon's `Alt` with the temporary promoted to `F.RES`/`F.CUR` in the frame (a clause suspends across an unbounded γ→β window; Icon's `|` inside one expression does not). Today the choice is `IR_DISJUNCTION` + per-clause `IR_MOVE_LABEL`/`IR_SUSPEND` (`lower_prolog.c:811,821`), `$unwind_nothrow` between clauses (`:840`), the cursor in a spine cell (`bb_disjunction.cpp:38-52`), retry addresses pushed to `g_pl_retry` (`bb_move_label.cpp:24-25`, `rt.c:1644`) and a 3-word CP to `g_pl_zf3_stack` (`bb_suspend.cpp:30-33`, `rt.c:1684-1708`). (iii) `F.CUR`, `F.RES`, `F.B`, `F.B0`, `F.LOG`, `F.G` — all activation-frame; nothing on a stack outside the box. **Source: Jcon `ir_a_Alt` (READ) for the shape; Kulas S:disj:4-6 (READ p.5) for the `OR(...)` memo = `F.CUR`; ECLiPSe's NEXT port (FETCHED) is the clause-step arm made visible.**

### B.4 Conjunction `A , B`
(i) Frameless. `C.α → A.α`; `A.γ → B.α`; `B.γ → C.γ`; `B.ω → A.β`; `A.ω → C.ω`; `C.β → B.β`. (ii) `ir_conjunction` (`irgen.icn:405-416`) verbatim; `/bounded & C.resume: goto right.resume`. Today: `thread_goals` (`lower_prolog.c:186-243`), flattening nested `,` (`:315-325`). (iii) Nothing of its own; the tokens `F.ACT[a]`, `F.ACT[b]` of its callees live in the enclosing frame. **Source: Proebsting 1997 § 4.3; Kulas S:conj (READ).**

### B.5 Disjunction `A ; B` (not if-then-else)
(i) Frameless but a CHOICE: `D.α → A.α`; `A.γ → D.γ` after banking `F.RESd := A.β`; `A.ω → B.α`; `B.γ → D.γ` banking `F.RESd := B.β`; `B.ω → D.ω`; `D.β → [F.RESd]` (indirect). Because D can be re-entered after the enclosing clause has run further goals, its cursor is NOT a spine temporary: it is one slot `F.RESd` in the enclosing call box's frame (Jcon's `ir_tmploc t` promoted). While D has an untried branch, the enclosing frame counts as a choice (`F.B := rbp`) — a disjunction never pushes a frame of its own (PZ-2 "homed per PF-1b"). (ii) Jcon `ir_a_Alt` (`irgen.icn:167-200`) exactly. Today the lowerer either inlines the branches with NO β (`lower_prolog.c:331-353`, `cx->beta = NULL` — an inline `;` cannot be resumed, which is a defect) or hoists a cut-free disjunction into a synthetic predicate `$disjN/k` (`dj_expand_node`, `:1028-1052`) so it gets a frame. (iii) `F.RESd` in the enclosing frame, one per disjunction node. `$disjN/k` hoisting becomes unnecessary once `F.RESd` exists; it stays lawful as an optimisation only where it wins. **Source: Jcon `ir_a_Alt` (READ); Kulas S:disj (READ).**

### B.6 Cut `!`
(i) A box K inside clause k of P. `K.α`: `F.B := F.B0`; `F.CUR := exhausted`; for each call site s to the LEFT of K in this clause with `F.ACT[s] ≠ 0`: promote that callee's `LOG` chain into `[F.B0].LOG` and clear `F.ACT[s]` (its β is now unreachable); for every choice frame C between old `F.B` and `F.B0` reached through those tokens, the same, transitively; `rsp := rbp` (all younger frames released physically — the WAM's `B ← B0` reclaims the stack). Then `K.γ`. `K.ω` is never entered from below (`K.β → K.ω` trampoline, as today `bb_cut.cpp:8-13`); the lowerer already rewires every ω to the right of the cut to `cut_ω` (`lower_prolog.c:191-208`, the `cx->cut_ω = uwf` at `:816`) — that compile-time part is kept ("free, correct", PZ-3). (ii) There is no Proebsting template for cut (Icon has none); the wiring is `ifstmt`'s gate idea applied to ω: after K, the clause's failure continuation is P's ω, statically. (iii) `F.B0` (the barrier IS the frame, as Lon ruled), `F.ACT[*]` tokens, the logs being promoted. No `g_resolve_cut_flag`. **Source: SWI `cut_call/cut_exit` (FETCHED) for the two hook points; Aït-Kaci 1991 § 5.11 `B ← B0` (RECORD) for the semantics; SICStus 3.7.1 (FETCHED) for the observable: a cut-away invocation is never re-entered.**

### B.7 If-then-else `C -> T ; E`
(i) Frameless. `I.α → C.α`; `C.γ → cut-of-C then T.α`; `C.ω → E.α`; `T.γ → I.γ`; `T.ω → I.ω`… no: `T.ω → I.ω` only after the local cut; `E.γ → I.γ`; `E.ω → I.ω`; `I.β → [gate]` where gate = `T.β` or `E.β` set by the branch taken (the ITE itself has no alternatives: C is committed). (ii) Proebsting's `ifstmt` (1997 READ p.4): *"E1's succeed and fail chunks set gate to the appropriate chunk's — either E1's or E2's — resume label"*, `ifstmt.resume: goto [ifstmt.gate]`. The "cut of C" is § B.6 applied with barrier = the enclosing frame's `F.B` at `I.α` (a local B0, one slot `F.B0i` per ITE). Today `lower_ite` (`lower_prolog.c:295-307`) wires C/T/E with an `IR_GOTO` funnel and puts the runtime guard `$no_throw_or_fail` on C's ω edge (`:301`); that guard leaves with C37 (§ B.10) because ω-with-ball simply bypasses E. (iii) `F.B0i` (16 B, enclosing frame) and the gate slot `F.RESi`. **Source: Proebsting 1997 § 4.5 (READ); Jcon `ir_a_If` (`irgen.icn:577-619`).**

### B.8 Negation `\+ G`
(i) Frameless, bounded. `N.α → G.α` with `F.B0n := F.B`; `G.γ → cut to F.B0n, undo everything G bound (its log entries sit in choices younger than F.B0n — promote nothing, UNDO them), → N.ω`; `G.ω → N.γ`; `N.β → N.ω`. (ii) Jcon `ir_a_Not` (`irgen.icn:142-160`): the inner expression is compiled `"always bounded"`, `expr.success → Not.failure`, `expr.failure → Not.success`, `/bounded & Not.resume: goto Not.failure`. Today `\+`/`not` lower to `lower_ite(G, fail, true)` (`lower_prolog.c:406`), `once` to `lower_ite(G, true, –)` (`:407`), `ignore` to `lower_ite(G, true, true)` (`:408`), `forall(C,A)` to `\+ (C, \+ A)` (`:414-417`) — all correct reductions to § B.7 and they stay. (iii) `F.B0n`; the undo on `G.γ` is the one place a SUCCESSFUL goal's bindings are undone — it is § B.6's promotion replaced by an unwind. **Source: Jcon `ir_a_Not` (READ); Kulas has no `\+` (pure Prolog).**

### B.9 `call/N` and meta-call (`G` a variable goal)
(i) A call box whose callee is unknown at compile time: `M.α`: build the goal term (spine scratch), hand it to the runtime compiler (EVAL/CODE path, THE RUNTIME-GOAL RULING in `ARCH-ENGINE.md`), receive the α address of a wired graph, then behave as an ordinary call box (§ B.3) with `F.ACT[m]` the token. `M.β`, `M.γ`, `M.ω` as B.3. (ii) Proebsting 1997 § 4.6 (READ p.4): *"Translating a function call that generates a sequence of values requires a mechanism for suspending and resuming a function invocation"* — Jcon's `ir_Call(closure, fn, args, resume)` / `ir_ResumeValue(target, closure, resume)` (`irgen.icn:360-404`): the callee's resumability is a CLOSURE VALUE the caller re-drives from its own β; in SCRIP the closure IS the retained frame (ARCH-LANGUAGES § Byrd-box refinements). Today: `$call/N` bridges (`lower_prolog.c:380-393`, `pl_ensure_call_bridge :1004`) → `IR_CALL_BUILTIN_GEN "$call"` + `IR_SUSPEND` (`:967-1003`) → `rt_pl_call_gen` (`by_name_dispatch.c:4750`) → the `plc_*` solver (21 static definitions, `:1125-4704`) — the runtime interpreter C36 deletes. (iii) Nothing beyond B.3; the compiled goal's blob is cached in the ζ-STANDING code slab (the pattern JIT's shape). **Source: Jcon `ir_a_Call` (READ); the runtime-compile rule is Lon's (`ARCH-ENGINE.md` § THE RUNTIME-GOAL RULING).**

### B.10 `catch/3` and `throw/1`
(i) Catch box H = a call box around Goal that is ALSO a choice frame while Goal has alternatives: `H.α`: `F.B0 := r10`, `F.B := rbp`, `F.BALL := 0`, → `Goal.α`. `Goal.γ → H.γ` (retained iff Goal retained; `r10` as B.3). `Goal.ω → H.βhandler`: if `r11 == 0` → plain failure → `H.ω`; else undo `F.LOG` (ISO: bindings since the catch/3 call are undone before the catcher is unified), `F.BALL := r11`, unify(Catcher, ball): fail → `r11 := ball`, `H.ω` (re-throw, ball still on the wire); succeed → `r11 := 0`, `F.B := F.B0`, → `Recovery.α` (`Recovery.γ → H.γ`, `Recovery.ω → H.ω`). `H.β → Goal.β` (catch/3 is transparent to backtracking). `throw(Ball)`: copy Ball to the heap (`rt_pl_throw_set` already copies, `unification.c:2236-2244`), `r11 := copy`, take ω. Every intermediate box's ω path leaves `r10:r11` alone (the undo helper is inline or parks them in the frame around a call). (ii) No Proebsting template (Icon errors are `&error` failures, Jcon § 7.2 READ p.18 trampolines Java exceptions into null returns); the shape is Tarau's BinProlog source-level `catch/throw` on continuations (RECORD): *"throw/1 ... returns a special exception pattern, while catch/3 stops the engine, calls a handler on matching exceptions or re-throws non-matching ones"*. Today: `$trail_mark` + `$catch_check` (`lower_prolog.c:361-379`), polled `rt_pl_throw_pending` at 9 sites in `by_name_dispatch.c` (`:2656,2665`, `rt_pl_call_gen` region) and the ball in `g_pl_throw_ball` (`unification.c:2234`). (iii) `F.BALL`, `F.B0`, `F.LOG` of the catch frame; the ball on the heap (escaper: it must outlive every frame between throw and catch). **Source: SWI exception port semantics (FETCHED: propagates frame by frame until caught); SICStus 4.5.1 Exception port (FETCHED); BinProlog (RECORD).**

### B.11 Unification `X = T` and THE TRAIL
(i) Frameless leaf: `U.α`: unify; bind → `U.γ`; mismatch → `U.ω` (after undoing the partial bindings this unify made — a unify is atomic w.r.t. the log: it logs as it binds and unwinds its own suffix on mismatch). `U.β → U.ω` (Kulas S:unif:2: `redo T1=T2 → fail T1=T2`). (ii) Proebsting's `LessThan` (1997 READ p.3): a leaf that may fail after its operands succeed. Today `$unify` / `$unify_lst` (`lower_prolog.c:149,169`), sunk inline (`bb_call_fn.cpp:232`, trail push inline at its `g_pl_trail` block). (iii) **The log rule (this is the variable alignment that decides everything else).** Binding cell `c` to value `v` inside frame F: `B := [rbp+0]` (`F.B`); if `c` is a stack cell and `addr(c) < [B+32]` (`B.HI`: `c` is younger than the youngest choice, it dies with it) → no log; else append `{c, *c}` to `[B+40]` (`B.LOG`), spilling to a heap chunk when the inline `K` are used. Heap cells (compound arguments on the GC heap) are always logged (no `HB`: the compacting collector moves the heap mark; measure before adding one — open question § F.5). Undo = walk `B.LOG` back to zero writing `old` into `addr`. Undo sites: β clause-step of the owning choice, its ω, `\+`'s success arm, the catch handler. **Promotion sites** (entries that must outlive their frame): cut (§ B.6) and nothing else — a bounded γ never promotes because a bounded activation never made `F.B` point at itself, so its bindings were logged in the older choice from the start (the WAM's rule, zero copying on the deterministic path). The per-activation "own log" of `ARCH-PROLOG-THREE-ZETAS.md` § 1 is therefore refined: a box logs into the youngest CHOICE's frame, which is its own frame exactly when it is that choice. GC: `LOG` entries are interior heap pointers → the `zls[]` kind map marks them `PTR_GC` (ARCH-ENGINE § GC). **Source: Aït-Kaci 1991 § 5.x conditional trailing (RECORD); `ARCH-LANGUAGES.md` § Known parity gaps (gprolog `Word_Needs_Trailing`, SWI `GTrail`); Kulas S:unif (READ).**

### B.12 Deterministic builtins — `is/2`, comparisons, type tests, `write/1`, …
(i) Frameless bounded leaves: `α` computes, `γ` or `ω`; `β → ω`. (ii) Proebsting `uminus`/`plus` for the arithmetic tree (`$ax_*` nodes, `lower_prolog.c:268-293`), `LessThan` for `$cmp_*` (`:521-531`), `literal` for `$is_v` (`:512-520`). (iii) ζ-SPINE only: argument staging cells at fixed offsets; the result DESCR in `rax:rdx` then a spine cell. **Source: Proebsting 1997 § 4.2-4.3 (READ).**

### B.13 Generator builtins — `between/3`, `member/2`, `append/3` (library), `clause/2`, `retract/1`, `sub_atom/5`
(i) A call box like § B.3 whose "clauses" are the generator's internal state: `F.CUR` holds the generator's counter/cursor (Proebsting's `to.I`), `F.B := rbp` while more values remain. `member/2`, `append/3` are ordinary predicates (library clauses) and get § B.3 unchanged. `clause/2` and `retract/1` iterate the dynamic database (§ B.15): `F.CUR` = the clause index in the ζ-STANDING table, re-validated on every β (logical update view: Kulas S:atom:2/4 memo the definition used at exit — we memo the snapshot generation number in `F.CUR`'s high half). (ii) Proebsting's `to(E1,E2)` (1997 READ p.3): *"The code below uses an extra code chunk as well as an additional temporary variable"* — `to.resume: to.I ← to.I+1; goto to.code`. Today each is a synthetic predicate wrapping `IR_CALL_BUILTIN_GEN` + `IR_SUSPEND` (`lower_prolog.c:395-405`, `:967-1003`) whose C body keeps its own state in the `resume` int64 (`rt_pl_dyn_iter_gen`, `unification.c:1928`). (iii) `F.CUR` (counter), `F.A[*]`, `F.LOG` as a choice. The C body becomes a value service that takes `(&F.CUR, args)` and returns the next value or FAIL — no state of its own. **Source: Proebsting 1997 § 4.4 (READ); Jcon `ToBy`/`Limit` (READ p.15).**

### B.14 All-solutions — `findall/3`, `bagof/3`, `setof/3` as a γ→β drive loop
(i) Box A with a frame: `A.α`: `F.ACC := empty`, `F.B0 := r10`, `F.B := rbp`, → `Goal.α`. `Goal.γ → collect`: copy Template to the heap, append to `F.ACC`, then FORCE FAILURE: → `Goal.β` (drive). `Goal.ω → finish`: undo `F.LOG`, `F.B := F.B0`, unify(Result, list(F.ACC)) → `A.γ` (bounded) or `A.ω`. `A.β → A.ω`. `bagof/setof`: same loop, then the free-variable grouping service (`rt_pl_bag_prep_cell :1229`, `rt_pl_bag_group_gen :1347`) yields groups — that outer generator is § B.13 with `F.CUR` over the groups. (ii) Jcon's `every E do B` (`irgen.icn:309-332`, Jcon paper READ p.25): `E.succeed → B.start`, `B.succeed → E.resume`, `B.fail → E.resume`, `E.fail → every.fail` — findall is `every` with `B` = "append and fail". Today: `$findall_new/$findall_add/$findall_result` and a forced-fail `IR_GOTO` to the goal's β (`lower_prolog.c:549-583`), `aggregate_all` the same shape (`:423-474`), and `pl_ll_maybe_lift` hoists non-trivial goals into `$faN/k` predicates (`:1253-1302`) so they have a β at all. (iii) `F.ACC` (two 16 B cells: head, tail) in A's frame; the collected copies on the heap (escapers by definition — they outlive the goal's frames); `F.LOG` is undone at finish so the template's bindings never leak. **Source: Jcon `ir_a_Every` (READ); Proebsting 1997 § 4.6 loops.**

### B.15 `assert/retract` and the dynamic database
(i) `assertz/1` etc.: bounded leaf (`α` → service → `γ`). ### B.16 DCG — `phrase/2,3`
(i) Nothing new: the translation `-->` → clauses with two extra list arguments happens in the parser (`prolog_parse.c:289-337` conjunction flattening, `:649-661` fresh `_S%d` variables and tail appends); `phrase(G, L, R)` lowers to a plain call of `G/(n+2)` (`lower_prolog.c:608-627`). ### B.17 `repeat/0`, `true/0`, `fail/0`, `once/1`, `forall/2`
`true`: `α → γ`, `β → ω` (Kulas S:true:1-2; today `IR_SUCCEED`, `lower_prolog.c:644`). `fail`: `α → ω` (S:fail; today `IR_GOTO ω`, `:645`). `repeat`: `α → γ`, `β → γ` — a choice with an infinite `F.CUR` (Jcon `RepAlt`, `irgen.icn:202-230`: `expr.failure → IndirectGoto(t)` where `t` alternates between `start` and `failure`; for `repeat` the resume simply re-succeeds), so the enclosing frame's `F.B := rbp` for as long as the repeat is live. `once/1` = `(G -> true)` § B.7; `forall/2` = `\+ (C, \+ A)` § B.8 (both already so lowered, `:407,:414`). **Source: Kulas (READ p.5); Jcon `ir_a_RepAlt` (READ).**

### B.18 Last call / tail recursion
(i) When the last goal L of clause k is a call and, at `L.α`, `F.B == F.B0` (this activation owns no live choice) and `rsp == rbp` (no retained callee frame lies below), P's frame can be REUSED: copy L's argument cells into `F.A[*]` (they may reference `F.G[*]` — the WAM's unsafe-variable rule: a local that escapes into the tail call's arguments is globalised to the heap first), pop nothing, jump `Callee.α` with P's own `F.γ/F.ω` wires and `F.LINK` — the callee inherits P's slot. Otherwise it is an ordinary call. (ii) Proebsting has no LCO (Icon procedures are not tail-recursive in the same sense); the WAM's `deallocate; execute` (Aït-Kaci 1991 § 5.8, RECORD). Today: none — recursion depth is bounded by frame stacking (`GOAL-PROLOG-100.md` PZ-9(a) "NO-LCO recursion cap"). (iii) The test reads two frame fields and `rsp`; nothing new. The static side (§ D) marks call boxes that are syntactically last and whose predicate is `deterministic` so the check is elided. **Source: Aït-Kaci 1991 (RECORD); Tarau's binarization (RECORD) is the continuation-passing statement of the same fact.**

## D. The debugging and analysis dividend

**Byrd's trace falls out of the wiring.** Every port transfer already passes ONE chokepoint: `x86_port_hook(site, port)` (`x86_asm.h:419`, defined `:1979`) is called by `x86_jmp`/`x86_jcc`/`x86_deflabel` for every α/β/γ/ω (`:427,:488,:499`; the port emitters `x86_alpha…x86_omega` at `:502-507`). A trace arm there — armed by `SCRIP_PL_TRACE=1`, print-only telemetry, the `SCRIP_ZETA_OMEGA_TRACE` carve-out — emits `call rt_port_trace(port, box_id)` with `rbp` live; the runtime prints `(N) D Port: goal` by reading `F.A[*]` through a per-graph RO descriptor blob (name, arity, the A offsets — the `.Lsig` blob shape, `xa_flat.cpp` CLASS-C prologue) and the depth D by walking `F.LINK` (`[rbp+kt-8]`). Exception vs Fail is `r11 ≠ 0` at an ω hook; `*EXIT` vs `EXIT` is `F.CUR` exhausted or not. The invocation number N is the one piece of program-lifetime state: a counter in ζ-STANDING (the root frame; the per-box cyc-proxy histogram of `ARCH-ENGINE.md` § Per-Box Profiling is the precedent). No interpreter, no second engine, and — the measured gap PZ-0 found — the ZSM ring that sees ZERO Prolog boxes today (`runtime_init.c`, `SCRIP_ZSM`) starts seeing them the moment Prolog boxes go through the same emitters.

**A port-hit profiler is the same hook with a counter instead of a print:** `count[box_id][port]++` in a ζ-STANDING array sized by the number of boxes (known at emit time). CALL counts per predicate, REDO/CALL ratio = how nondeterministic a predicate is in practice, FAIL/EXIT ratio = how often indexing would have saved a clause try — the three numbers the WAM literature tunes on.

**Static analyses the port graph enables** (each is a compile-time property of the wired graph, none needs a runtime bit):
- **Determinism** = Proebsting's `bounded`: a box is bounded iff its β chunk is unreachable — no clause after the current one can be a candidate (§ B.13 indexing leaves one), every body goal is bounded, no `;` or `repeat` without a following cut. Today `pl_det_compute` (`lower_prolog.c:1202-1217`, `pl_det_pred_ok :1191`) is a syntactic whitelist (`:1139-1140`) that stamps `deterministic = 1` (`:1228`); the port-graph version replaces the whitelist with reachability of β and is language-blind (the emitter's `op_beta_dead` already elides a dead β trampoline, `x86_asm.h:504`). A bounded predicate emits no β chunk, no `F.CUR`, no `F.RES`, and its γ releases the frame — the whole cost of choice disappears where the program never chooses.
- **First-argument indexing as a compile-time choice cut**: the `$ix_g` guards (`lower_prolog.c:827-838`, sunk at `bb_call_fn.cpp:379`) become the WAM's `switch_on_term` at `P.α`: the A0 tag selects the candidate SET (a compile-time bitmask per key), `F.CUR` iterates only that set, and when the set is a singleton `F.B` is not set to `rbp` — the call is bounded at run time even when the predicate is not bounded statically. The trace shows this as an EXIT without `*`.
- **Last-call detection**: a call box that is syntactically last in its clause AND whose enclosing predicate is bounded needs no `F.B == F.B0` check (§ B.18); the emitter marks it and the frame-reuse jump is unconditional.
- **Cut analysis**: a clause whose cut is the first body goal (`p(X) :- !, …`) makes every later clause unreachable for calls that unify its head — the candidate set after indexing is pruned statically, which is what turns a "guard-complement pair" (`pl_det_guard_comp :1176`) from a special case into an instance of the general rule.

## F. Open questions for Lon (five, each with the recommended answer)

1. **Where does a binding log?** — bind-time into the youngest CHOICE frame's `LOG` via the per-frame `F.B` slot (the WAM's rule; zero copying on the deterministic path; copying only at cut), versus the literal reading of "each activation's own log" with promotion at every bounded γ. **Recommend the first**; § B.11 states why the literal reading breaks (a deterministic callee's binding of a caller cell must outlive the callee's frame).
2. **Is a log chunk a lawful escaper?** — when a retained choice's `LOG` outgrows its inline `K` entries, the overflow goes to heap cells (24 B each, `PTR_GC`-mapped). **Recommend yes**: it is exactly the "retained across a γ and grows without bound" case the criterion names, and the alternative (a fixed cap) is a REFUSE.
3. **The ball's carrier** — ⛔ `r11` is taken (BB node id) and `r10` is taken (statement number), so the choice is a free wire register from the plane versus a `BALL` slot in every catch frame. **ANSWERED by Lon's 2026-09-02 grant: the ball rides `r15`, callee-saved, no frame slot**: no per-frame cost, one register test at catch boxes only; intermediate ω paths must preserve `r10:r11` around helper calls (an emitter rule, gated by `test_gate_port_exit_value_contract.sh`'s sibling).
4. **The ζ-STANDING tables** (dynamic database, `nb_setval`, settable flags, the proc table) reached through root-frame slots — is a root-frame slot "not a global" under NO-NEW-GLOBALS, or does each need the banner ask? **Recommend: root-frame slots are ζ-STANDING by the law's own name and need no banner**; the page still lists them so the count is honest.
5. **Heap cells always logged (no HB)** — the compacting collector moves the heap mark, so the WAM's `HB` test is not available for free; log every heap-cell binding and measure on van Roy before designing a generation stamp. **Recommend accept for PZ-4, revisit under PZ-10 with a number.**
