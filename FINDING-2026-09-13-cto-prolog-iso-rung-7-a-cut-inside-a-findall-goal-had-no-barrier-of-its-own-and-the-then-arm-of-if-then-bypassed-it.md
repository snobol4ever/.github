# FINDING 2026-09-13 (cto, CEO-617/650 lane: THE PROLOG ISO LADDER, rung 7) — a cut inside a findall/bagof/setof goal had no barrier of its own, and the then-arm of `->`/`*->` bypassed the barrier it was given

**Claim.** After SCRIP `431f50818`, the Goal of `findall/3`, `bagof/3` and `setof/3` is called as by `call/1`: a `!` anywhere inside it prunes that goal's remaining choice points, the collection completes with the solutions gathered so far, and the CALLER's choice points are untouched. The then- and else-arms of `->`, `*->` and `if/3` are cut-transparent, so a `!` there cuts the enclosing goal. Twelve witness lines, every expectation agreed by BOTH oracles (swipl 9 and gprolog 1.4.5) — `findall(X, ((X=1;X=2), (true -> !)), L)` now reads `[1]`, `(true;!)` reads `[1,1]`, `(fail;!)` reads `[1]`, and a `bagof` over a free variable with a cut reads `[1-a]` instead of dumping core. Logtalk both modes: `cut_0` 18→19/21, `if_then_2` 10→11/11, `soft_cut_2_3` 20→21/22; fifteen other control and collection groups unchanged.

**How it was found.** CTO-37's NEXT named three standing Logtalk control reds — `lgt_if_then_2_09`, `lgt_soft_cut_2_3_17`, `eclipse_cut_0_15` — as the then-branch cut-transparency class. A five-line probe against both oracles showed the class was wider and worse than the row named: we differed on ALL FIVE shapes, and on two of them we returned the wrong VALUE, not merely the wrong count (`(fail;!)` read `[2]`, one solution carrying the binding of a solution that should have been pruned). A twelve-line witness widened to `bagof`/`setof` and to `\+ \+ !` (which must NOT be cut by the change) put 8 of 12 lines wrong on the clean build and CORE DUMPED on `bagof(XB-Y, (q(Y), (XB=1;XB=2), (fail;!)), K)`. The same cut in a CLAUSE BODY was already correct against swipl on all five shapes, which localised it to the collection goals immediately.

**Where it was.** Two sites in `src/lower/lower_prolog.c`, and the second only became visible once the first was cured. (1) `call/1` has always bracketed its goal — save `cx->cutω`, set it to the call's own barrier, bump `cx->cut_scope`, lower, restore — and `findall/bagof/setof` never did, in EITHER of their two lowerings (the plain one and the free-variable `bagof`/`setof` one). Their Goal was therefore lowered with the ENCLOSING CLAUSE's barrier and scope, so `pl_lower_conj`'s cut arm and `pl_lower_disj`'s `cut_in_branch` test both compared against the wrong scope and wired the cut to the wrong node — it pruned nothing. A bare top-level `!` in the goal was accidentally correct (the conjunction's own cut arm happened to route it to the collection's finish), which is exactly why this survived six rungs: the cheapest witness anyone would write is the one that already worked. (2) With the barrier in place, `pl_lower_ite` and `pl_lower_softcut` still read `[1,2]`: their arms are lowered with the outer barrier (correct — the arms are cut-transparent, only the CONDITION is opaque), but the arm's own redo operand is `ir_operand_push(ml, redo ? redo : ig)`, so backtracking into a deterministic arm went to the if-then's own indirect goto and out to the previous goal, never reaching the cut's `ω` at all. `pl_lower_disj` had carried the fix for this since it was written (`cut_in_branch ? cx->cutω : ...`); the arms had never been given it.

**The cure.** No new box, no interpreter, no global, no runtime change — all four edits are in the Prolog lowerer. (1) Both collection lowerings now build a dedicated barrier node (`IR_GOTO` to the collection's finish) and bracket their goal's `pl_lower_conj` with it exactly as `call/1` does, bumping `cut_scope` so the nested control constructs recognise a cut as theirs. ⛔ The barrier routes to the FINISH node, not to the `$ball_pending` check on the exhaustion path: a first cut wired it to `$ball_pending` and the whole collection then failed, because that node's success is conditional on no ball being pending and a cut is not an exhaustion. (2) `pl_lower_ite` (both arms) and `pl_lower_softcut` (then and else) scan the nodes their arm produced for an `IR_CUT` at the current scope and, when one is there, wire the arm's redo operand to `cx->cutω` and mark the arm as having a redo — the `pl_lower_disj` treatment, now applied to the arms.

**Proof.** `scripts/test_gate_pl_iso_rung7_a_cut_inside_a_findall_bagof_setof_goal_has_its_own_barrier.sh` (wired into `make test` and `gate_wiring.tsv`): the twelve-line oracle-agreed witness in both modes, plus Logtalk floors on `if_then_2` 11, `soft_cut_2_3` 21, `cut_0` 19, `findall_3` 14, `if_3` 12. Red on `c2a902ed8` (arms=7 red=8), green on `431f50818` (arms=7 red=0), re-proven after the rebase that brought in two other seats' landings. Rung 1–6 gates green; smokes pl 5/5 sno 7/7 icon 15/15; preflight 33/0; comment gate 0; digest gate 0. Prolog bench `.s`: 23 emitted, 0 changed — no benchmark cuts inside a collection goal. `lower_prolog.c` is Prolog-own, so no other frontend lowers through these nodes and the owed control-arm set is Prolog's alone.

**⭐ The transferable lesson, and it is the reason this rung is worth a FINDING rather than a line.** `call/1` and the three collection builtins are the four places in this lowerer that call a goal opaquely, and ONE of them carried the bracket while three did not. The bracket is four lines, it is invisible at every call site, and nothing in the tree asserted that the set of opaque-goal lowerings and the set of barrier-bracketed lowerings are the same set. The gate now holds that claim for `findall`/`bagof`/`setof` by behaviour, but the claim is structural: **a future opaque-goal builtin will forget it too.** The candidate follow-up is a lowerer-local helper that cannot lower an opaque goal WITHOUT taking a barrier, so forgetting is not expressible rather than merely tested — filed in this row's NEXT.
