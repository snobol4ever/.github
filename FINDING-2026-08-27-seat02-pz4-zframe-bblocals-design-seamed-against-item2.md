## 1. THE CURRENT MECHANISM, exactly, file:line

Prolog's multi-clause retry does **not** resume the same activation — it **recreates a fresh one** and marshals three values across the old-frame-death → new-frame-birth gap through five process-wide globals (`rt.c:1714-1719`: `g_pl_zf_pending_cursor/_tm_lo/_tm_hi/_tm_off/_cursor_off`, plus a `g_pl_zf_target_pcall_top` that is written and reset but **never read anywhere** — grep confirms zero consumers; a stub for an ownership check that was never wired up).

The retry entry (`bcps_spine_gen_arm`, `bb_call_proc_staged.cpp:813-844`, tagged `PL-FR-4 zframe β` in its own comment) does, in order:
1. `rt_pl_cp_pop3` pops `(tm_lo, tm_hi, cont)` off a **global LIFO array** `g_pl_zf3_stack` (`rt.c:1688-1713`) into the caller's own call-site bookkeeping slot.
2. `rt_pl_zf_resume_set(cursor, tm_lo, tm_hi, tm_off, cursor_off)` stashes all five into the global mailbox (`rt.c:1721-1729`).
3. Args are re-staged and the callee is **called again from its α port** — a brand-new activation.
4. That activation's prologue calls `rt_jmp_frame_lexprep2(fb, ...)` (`xa_flat.cpp:286-297`, guarded by `g_emit.zframe_graph && g_emit.flat_lex`), which (`rt.c:1629-1647`) `memset`s the whole frame, then **if** a pending cursor is set, copies it to `fb+cursor_off`/`fb+tm_off`/`fb+tm_off+8` **and unconditionally mirrors `tm_lo`/`tm_hi` into `fb+0`/`fb+8`** regardless of whether a cursor was pending, clearing the global cursor after consuming it.

**`cursor_off` and `tm_off` are not hardcoded** — they come from `zls_g_resume_by_name`/`zls_g_pl_zf_trail_mark_off_by_name` (`bb_call_proc_staged.cpp:712,715`), which are **already** real, per-graph, `zls_field`-registered offsets (`zeta_storage.c:447-459` for `resume_off`; `scrip_ir.c:285-297` + `zeta_storage.c:759-761` for `pl_trail_mark_off`, which piggybacks on the ordinary result-slot the compiler already gives the `$trail_mark` builtin call — clever, and already live). **Only the unconditional `fb+0`/`fb+8` mirror write is unregistered** — it goes through no `zls_field` call at all.

Immediately adjacent in the same function, Icon's generator retry (`zf_resume`, `bb_call_proc_staged.cpp:801-812`) does something structurally different and much cheaper: **no recreation, no globals** — it reads a saved frame handle straight out of the caller's own `FRQ(act+8)` slot, calls `rt_gen_get_cont`/restores `rsp` from it, and `jmp`s directly back into the retained activation. This is the shape PZ-4 wants. It sits three branches away from Prolog's mechanism in the literal same function.

## 2. Why Prolog can't just copy `zf_resume` verbatim, and why that's not a shortcut waiting to happen

Two independent reasons, both load-bearing:

**(a) Clause-retry semantics differ from generator-resume semantics.** A generator resumes the *same* execution point with the *same* locals. A Prolog clause retry needs the *next clause's own* head-unification variables — those must be fresh per attempt. What must persist across the retry is much narrower: which clause to try next (the cursor) and the trail depth at entry (so `β` unwinds to the right mark). ARCH §5 already says this in words: *"β (recede): retry: unwind the trail to this box's mark (R12), try the next clause."* One retained activation, R12-unwind between attempts, fresh spine-tier scratch per clause — never a fresh activation. That is a real design (§3 below), not "make Prolog look like Icon."

**(b) Icon's own `zf_resume`, in its *current* (non-`icn_genframe2`) form, has the identical disease PZ-4 is fighting.** Look at `bb_call_proc_staged.cpp:763-766`: without `icn_genframe2()` armed, the "retained frame handle" banked into `FRQ(act+8)` is just `rsp` — a raw stack address, exactly the "fixed-rsp-relative continuation slot" shape all four PZ-4 convergence FINDINGs name as the crash class (stale after unbounded intervening stack growth). Icon gets away with it more often only because a single generator's own resume window is usually shallower than what a multi-clause Prolog predicate does under backtracking with nested calls. **Copying `zf_resume`'s current legacy shape into Prolog would not cure the wall — it would relocate the exact same bug into a mechanism that looks fixed on a simple witness and reproduces the whole convergence class the moment a real program nests deeper.** This is the concrete, code-level reason "seamed against item 2, not shortcut around it" is the right instruction, not just caution for its own sake: the *safe* version of `zf_resume`'s shape only exists once `icn_genframe2()`'s RBP-relative addressing (item 1, landed, gated) is joined by item 2's actual host-frame carve — neither of which Prolog has today (ceo's own ruling on this row: `grep -c rbp` over `main` returns zero; there is no RBP frame to carve into).

## 3. The target design (for once item 2 lands) — concrete, not a restatement of the ARCH doc

Retire the global mailbox entirely (`g_pl_zf3_stack`, `g_pl_zf_pending_*`, `rt_jmp_frame_lexprep2`, `rt_pl_cp_push3`/`pop3`, `rt_pl_zf_resume_set`/`_clear`) — ARCH §5's own line, now traced to the exact functions it means: *"Do not fix that no-op by writing a heap-aware frame seed... make it BB LOCALS at the activation-frame zeta, and the function has nothing left to do."* Concretely:

- The activation frame (once retained via item 2's host carve, PZ-4 clause (b)/(c)) keeps its own resume cursor and trail-mark fields **in place** — they are already `zls_field`-registered per-graph offsets (`resume_off`, `pl_trail_mark_off`); nothing new needs allocating for *these two*.
- Retry (`bcps_spine_gen_arm`'s `pl_zf_resume` branch) stops popping a global stack and recreating an activation. It becomes the same shape as the adjacent `zf_resume` branch: read the retained frame's own saved base (the Prolog-specific analogue of `FRQ(act+8)`, sourced from an **item-2-carved, RBP-relative address**, never a raw `rsp` snapshot), unwind the trail to the frame's own saved mark (R12, per ARCH §5), and `jmp` directly to the frame's own `resume_off` slot. No new call, no global mailbox, no frame recreation — the retry IS a jump back into a frame that never stopped existing.
- The one field that genuinely has no home yet is the **"already suspended" discriminator** — today a bare, unregistered `fb+0`/`fb+8` write with no `zls_field` entry (§1, §4). In the target design this becomes a real named field allocated the same way (`zls_field` + a `pl_susp_off`-style accessor mirroring `zls_g_pl_zf_trail_mark_off_by_name`), read directly from the retained frame rather than written fresh on every recreation — because there is no more recreation to seed.
- The one call site that changes: `bcps_spine_gen_arm`'s `pl_zf_resume` branch (`bb_call_proc_staged.cpp:813-840`). Everything upstream of it (arg staging, the α-port unify dispatch, `resume_off`/`pl_trail_mark_off` allocation) is reused unchanged.

**This is deliberately not implemented this pass.** Building any part of the "restore-and-jump" retry now, before item 2 gives Prolog a stable frame-base equivalent to `FRQ(act+8)`, has no safe address to restore from — it would have to fake one (a raw `rsp` snapshot, i.e. exactly Icon's legacy fragility, §2b) or do nothing. Per hq_P's ruling already on this row and ceo's design ruling, that is the "temporary promotion" both explicitly forbid. What's real and load-bearing about this pass is narrowing *where* the seam is to one function and one missing field, with the reasoning traced to source rather than asserted.

## 4. One inherited claim checked against real compiler output, and partially refined

Seat07's ADDENDUM 3 (linked from this row) reads: *"`[fb+0]`/`[fb+8]` is not an exclusively-reserved slot in `zls_build`'s layout; ordinary per-node output clobbers it before the clause's own SUSPEND dispatch reads it."* Per this project's own re-verification discipline, checked directly: compiled the row's canonical witness (`fact(a).fact(b).fact(c). main :- fact(X),write(X),nl,fail;true.`) with `--dump-zeta`.

**Structural fact, confirmed from `zeta_storage.c:421-423` and matching the dump exactly:** `base` (where a graph's own per-node slots start) is `s0 ? 16+... : 0`, and `s0` is forced true by the *same* reachable-`IR_SUSPEND` scan that grants `resume_off` (`zeta_storage.c:421-422,448`). **This means any graph that owns a `resume_off` is structurally guaranteed `base ≥ 16` — offset 0/8 can never be an ordinary per-node slot in that same graph.** The dump bears this out: `fact/1`'s own graph and `main`'s body both show `base=16` with clean scope ranges (`[16..464)`, `[16..496)`); offset 0 is unused in both. The **only** graph in this witness with `base=0` (an `IR_DISJUNCTION` result sitting at `+0`) is the top-level `;` wrapper, which has `resume=-1` — it does not own a `resume_off` and is not itself a `pl_zf_resume` retry target.

**So the collision, if it is real, is not in the graph that owns the cursor it would be colliding with — my own witness doesn't reproduce it.** This doesn't mean ADDENDUM 3 is wrong: their repro may be a different witness shape (a 0-arity multi-clause predicate whose SUSPEND is somehow not `rb[]`-reachable at the point checked, or a collision against a *different* frame's bytes than I've modeled, or the unconditional mirror write landing in a caller's frame rather than the callee's). **Flagged honestly rather than papered over: re-check against their specific repro before either fixing or retiring this claim.** What *is* independently confirmed, and is reason enough on its own to give the suspended-flag a real field per §3: the write is unconditional and goes through zero `zls_field` registration, so nothing in the allocator's bookkeeping knows those bytes are spoken for — that is a live hazard by construction even where this witness doesn't happen to trip it.
