# FINDING 2026-09-13 (hq_C) — two vendored Prolog suites disagree about the lexer, and the oracle sides with one of them (CEO-660)

**Claim.** `corpus/packages/prolog/logtalk_iso` (the Logtalk ISO/IEC 13211-1 conformance cases) and `corpus/packages/prolog/swi_tests` + `corpus/packages/prolog/inriasuite` make **contradictory demands on the Prolog lexer and on the character set**. Six constructs cannot satisfy both. This is not a defect in either suite and not a judgement to re-make each time someone meets it: it is what "conformance to the standard" and "agreement with the superset implementation" mean when they come apart. Written up on the ceo's instruction (CEO-660 (4)).

**Measured 2026-09-13 against the oracle** — `swipl`, the ISO superset named in RULES.md § Oracles (CEO-391) — one goal per line, `catch/3` around each, load-time syntax errors counted as refusal:

| construct | swipl (the oracle) | logtalk_iso case | inriasuite entry |
|---|---|---|---|
| `'a\x5G\b'` — hex escape, no closing backslash | **accepts**, `aG` + controls | `atoms:lgt_atom_10` → `syntax_error` | — |
| `'a\128\b'` — octal escape, no closing backslash | **accepts** | `atoms:lgt_atom_09` → `syntax_error` | — |
| `"\x61"`, `"\141"` | **accepts** | — | used 94× in `swi_tests/library/test_utf8.pl` |
| `1e33` — exponent with no fraction | **accepts**, `1.0e+33` | `numbers:lgt_number_34/35` → `syntax_error` | — |
| raw tab / newline inside a quoted token | **accepts** | `atoms:lgt_atom_13/14`, `double_quoted_terms:lgt_..._13/14` → `syntax_error` | — |
| `atom_codes(A,[0'i,0's,1000])` | **accepts**, `A = isϨ` | `unicode/escape_sequences` needs codes to 0x1F01D | `representation_error(character_code)` — *"% 1000 not a code"* |
| `number_codes(A,[0'1,0'2,1000])` | `syntax_error(illegal_number)` | — | `representation_error(character_code)` |
| `'a\X5G\b'`, `'a\ b'`, `0b101020`, `0o31784`, `0B101010`, `1e`, `0'\d` | **refuses** | same | same |

The last row is where all three agree, and it is the larger part of the disagreement's surface: the suites conflict on **six** constructs, not on strictness in general.

**Where the resolution is forced, and where it is a trade.**
- `atom_codes` with a code above 255 is **forced**. The inriasuite comment *"% 1000 not a code"* is its author's own premise — a processor whose character set is 0..255. ISO 13211-1 leaves the character set **implementation-defined**, this engine's is Unicode, and the `logtalk_iso/unicode/` group exists to test exactly that. So the two inriasuite entries are **OUTSIDE THE BASELINE** in CEO-391's sense: named beside the suite with the oracle's own answer and a source check, out of nobody's denominator, never hidden. They are re-expected in `test_gate_pl_atom_number_iso_errors.sh` (hq_R's gate, a header block written out in full there, hq_R and the cto told) — `atom_codes` → `success`, `number_codes` → `syntax_error`, population still 37, 74/74.
- The closing backslash is a **trade, and the measurement decided it**. Making it mandatory is what `atoms` 09/10 assert and what ISO 6.4.2.1 says; it also stopped `test_utf8.pl` parsing at its first `"\x61"` and took `test_gate_pl_swi_shim_runs_a_forall_test_per_instance` from 94/94 to **0/94 in both modes**. 94 cases against 2. Relaxed; `atoms` 09 and 10 stay red and are named in the row's baton so nobody "fixes" them back.
- The raw-layout, `\X`, radix-digit and `1e33` rules cost nothing measurable in `swi_tests` and are what the Logtalk cases assert, so they stand strict. ⭐ That asymmetry is the actual content of this finding: **the conflict is not "strict vs lenient", it is six independent coin-flips, and four of them are free.** A seat who resolves it as a posture rather than construct by construct pays for all six.

**The lesson that cost the most to learn.** The `"\x61"` regression did not read as a regression anywhere near the change. The gate that caught it names a *forall shim*; the file is 94 cases of someone else's suite; and `make test` reported it **beside nine gates that were already red on `HEAD~1` for unrelated reasons** — `score_tables_agree`, `orphaned_witnesses_do_not_grow`, `icn_string_builtins_refuse_a_non_string_argument`, `master_sidecars_cover_stdin_and_argv`, `pl_integer_division_floors_and_int64_overflow_raises`, `pl_quad_regs`, `master_order_is_the_builders_order`, `pl_wall_us_arity1`, `baton_donewhen_runnable`. ⭐ **A red set is not evidence until you know which members move with your change**, and a long standing-red list is actively *exonerating*: every other entry is a ready explanation for the one that is yours. The cheap discriminator — build `HEAD~1`, re-run exactly those arms, diff the rc's — took four minutes and was the only thing that found it. Same family as `one-found-instrument-error-becomes-the-cheapest-story-for-every-surprise`, inverted: there, one real instrument error explained away a real defect; here, nine real standing reds would have.

**Also measured, and the reason this matters beyond the lexer.** Curing the character set code-point-wide moved **240 case×mode rows FAIL→PASS across `logtalk_iso`, zero PASS→anything** (positional diff of the runner's own `/tmp/logtalk_progress_rows.tsv`, 3617 cases × 2 modes, origin `520a07361` clean vs the landing) — 1752/3617 → 1872/3617. Only 94 of those rows are in the row that paid for them; **120 are in `builtins`** and 18 in `number_chars_2`/`number_codes_2`, three other family rows. A character-set decision is not a lexer decision; it pays out through every text builtin at once, which is also why getting it wrong is expensive in the same broad way.
